What Lasts
6th Grade ELA · Shakespeare Stories
50 pointsWord file (.docx)Reflection: written or recorded
Of everything you read this year, what lasts? Choose one idea, find it in three characters from three different books, and argue that it connects them. Then share a short reflection on what you carry forward, written or recorded. The choice is yours.

Steps

1
Choose one idea and three characters from three different books. The four ideas and their questions:
Heroism: what does it look like to do the right thing when it costs something?
Self-Sacrifice: what does a person give up and receive in return?
Happiness: how do the characters discover what happiness requires?
Duty: what does a person owe, to whom, and what happens when it is paid?
The Choose What Lasts lesson has a gallery of characters for each idea. Another character is welcome if you can make the case.
2
Open with a topic sentence that states your argument: a claim someone could disagree with, not a list of names.
3
Give one specific moment for each character: something the character does, not a general trait. Connect the characters with transitions such as likewise, similarly, in contrast, or while.
4
Write one sentence that begins "What all three of these characters share is …".
5
Close with a sentence on what the idea means and why stories keep returning to it. Saving your main point for the last words of the sentence works well.
6
Share a reflection, "What I Carry Forward": one thing you carry forward from this year's reading, the character who helped you see it, and why it matters now. The character does not have to be one from your essay. Choose one way: write 60 to 100 words, or record yourself speaking for 2 to 3 minutes on any device.

Requirements

The essay is 200 to 250 words, in Word, with the title in Heading 1 and the body in Times New Roman 12 or Calibri 11.

Three characters from three different books. Two characters from the same play count as one book.

Name each book and its author in your sentences.

A written reflection goes under the essay in the same Word file. A recorded reflection is saved as an audio or video file and uploaded here with the Word file.

Four of the books are still under copyright: St. George and the Dragon, King Arthur and His Knights of the Round Table, The Adventures of Robin Hood, and The Bronze Bow. Tell their moments in your own words. Short quotations are fine from "The Tempest," "Much Ado About Nothing," and "The Golden Touch."
