✏️ Grammar: Proper and Common Nouns
Step 1 of 3 this week: learn the rule.

A noun names a person, place, thing, or idea. A common noun names any member of a group and stays lowercase: outlaw, forest, sheriff, king, town. A proper noun names one specific person, place, or thing and always begins with a capital letter: Robin Hood, Sherwood Forest, Nottingham, King Richard, England.

Ask whether the word is the name of one particular thing, or just a kind of thing. “A forest” is any forest. “Sherwood Forest” is one named place. A title like sheriff is common on its own but becomes part of a proper noun inside a full name, as in “the Sheriff of Nottingham.”
