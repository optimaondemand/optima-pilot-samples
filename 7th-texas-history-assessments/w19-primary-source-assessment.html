Source A
President James K. Polk, Special Message to Congress on Mexican Relations, May 11, 1846 (excerpt)
In the meantime, we have tried every effort at reconciliation. The cup of forbearance had been exhausted even before the recent information from the frontier of the Del Norte. But now, after reiterated menaces, Mexico has passed the boundary of the United States, has invaded our territory and shed American blood upon the American soil. She has proclaimed that hostilities have commenced, and that the two nations are now at war.
Source B
Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo, February 2, 1848 (excerpts)
Article V. The boundary line between the two Republics shall commence in the Gulf of Mexico, three leagues from land, opposite the mouth of the Rio Grande, otherwise called Rio Bravo del Norte...
Article VIII. Mexicans now established in territories previously belonging to Mexico, and which remain for the future within the limits of the United States, as defined by the present treaty, shall be free to continue where they now reside, or to remove at any time to the Mexican Republic, retaining the property which they possess in the said territories...
Article XII. In consideration of the extension acquired by the boundaries of the United States, as defined in the fifth article of the present treaty, the Government of the United States engages to pay to that of the Mexican Republic the sum of fifteen millions of dollars.
Question 1

Polk says Mexico shed American blood "upon the American soil." What did Polk assume about the land where the fighting happened, and why would Mexico have disagreed?

Question 2

Article V of the treaty places the boundary at the Rio Grande. Suppose the treaty had instead placed it at the Nueces River. How might that have changed Texas's position, and would Texans have accepted it? Explain your reasoning.
