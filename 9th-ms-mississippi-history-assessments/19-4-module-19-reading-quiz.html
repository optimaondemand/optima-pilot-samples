QUESTION 1
In the secession debate, support for waiting was strongest in the northeastern hills and the pine woods, where fewer families owned enslaved people. What does this pattern suggest?
A. Everyone in the hill country wanted to stay in the Union no matter what happened.
B. The hill country had no cotton farms at all, so its delegates took no side in the debate.
C. Support for waiting was stronger where fewer people owned enslaved people.
D. The convention chose its delegates by lot, so they did not stand for any group of voters.
QUESTION 2
Abraham Lincoln promised not to end slavery where it already existed, yet many Mississippi leaders still feared his election. Which belief best explains the fear?
A. They thought he would tax cotton so heavily that planters could no longer make a profit.
B. They thought he would draft Mississippians into the Union army as soon as he took office.
C. They thought he would lead the Northern states out of the Union to form a new country.
D. They believed slavery had to keep spreading, and he opposed its spread into the territories.
QUESTION 3
The Declaration of Causes says, "For far less cause than this, our fathers separated from the Crown of England." Why would the delegates compare themselves to the founders?
A. To present secession as a justified act in the tradition of American independence.
B. To admit that the delegates knew they were breaking the law and meant to pay the price.
C. To ask Britain for help in the war that the delegates expected to come with the North.
D. To argue that the Constitution had been repealed by the Northern states.
QUESTION 4
Some people say the Civil War was about "states' rights." The Declaration of Causes complains that Northern states used their own power to block a federal law that protected slavery. What does that complaint suggest?
A. The document never mentions any state and speaks only of the nation as a whole in its text.
B. The document defended slavery, and it argued for states' rights only when that helped.
C. The document praised Northern state governments for protecting the liberty of their citizens.
D. The document argued that the federal government was too weak to collect taxes in the South.
QUESTION 5
The Anaconda Plan called for the Union to take control of the Mississippi River. Why would that help defeat the Confederacy?
A. It would give the Union every Southern railroad running across the Confederate states.
B. It would end the blockade of Southern ports, which no longer served any purpose.
C. It would force Mississippi to rejoin the Union as soon as the river was opened.
D. It would split the Confederacy in two and cut Texas, Louisiana, and Arkansas off.
QUESTION 6
Confederate commander Albert Sidney Johnston was killed on the first day at Shiloh, and many historians think the battle might have gone differently had he lived. What does this show about how wars unfold?
A. Armies never recover after they lose a commander in the middle of a large battle.
B. Commanders rarely matter to the outcome of a battle once the armies are in the field.
C. Outcomes can turn on what happens to one leader, so they are not fixed in advance.
D. Battles are decided only by the number of soldiers each side brings to the field.
QUESTION 7

A. They left northern Mississippi in Union hands and weakened the river's defenders.
B. They ended the Union blockade of Southern ports and opened the coast to trade.
C. They convinced Mississippi to leave the Confederacy and ask for peace with the North.
