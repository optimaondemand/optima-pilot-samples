UNIT 1 • WEEK 1 • READING

Your Digital Identity

Every account you create, every photo tagged with your name, every comment you leave is a brushstroke on a portrait that future teachers, coaches, and employers can see. This week you'll learn what digital identity really is — and start taking deliberate control of yours.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

1
Define digital identity and identify the layers (profile, content, behavior, data trails) that make it up.
2
Distinguish between the identity you curate and the identity others and algorithms infer from your patterns.
3
Explain how everyday online actions — posts, likes, searches, tags — build a persistent reputation over time.
4
Identify traits of a resilient digital identity and audit one element of your own online presence.
01
Section 1 · What Is Digital Identity?

More than your username — it's the version of you the internet sees

Open Canvas, your school email, Instagram, TikTok, your gaming account, your YouTube history. Each of those services holds a small piece of who you are. Together, those pieces add up to your digital identity — the version of you that other people, search engines, and computer systems can recognize without ever meeting you in person.

Digital identity has three ingredients on every platform you use:

📘 Vocabulary

Digital identity — the picture other people and systems form of you based on what you do, post, and search online.

Persona — a deliberate version of yourself you present in a specific space (your school account vs. your gaming account vs. your art portfolio).

Digital footprint — the trail of data you leave behind every time you click, post, message, or search.

02
Section 2 · The Signals You Send

Five kinds of signals — and almost all of them are public

Most students think "my profile" is their digital identity. The truth is, profile basics are just one layer of five. Each layer below broadcasts something about you to anyone who looks — and each one is searchable, screenshot-able, and often permanent.

🪪1 · Profile Basics
What it is: Username, profile photo, bio, banner, links. Why it matters: This is the version of you that loads first — and first impressions form fast, often within seconds.
💬2 · Posts & Comments
What it is: The words you choose to say in public — captions, replies, tweets, threads. Why it matters: Tone, grammar, and topic together reveal what you care about and how you treat people.
❤️3 · Reactions
What it is: Likes, shares, follows, reposts. Why it matters: Even when you don't say a word, who and what you boost reveals your taste, values, and circle of trust.
🏷️4 · Tags & Mentions
What it is: What other people say about you and link to your name — photos you're in, group chats, comments under your handle. Why it matters: You don't fully control this layer. Other people's posts become part of your identity too.
📡5 · Data Trails
What it is: Login times, device used, location pings, scroll patterns, search history. Why it matters: Invisible to you, fully visible to algorithms. Ads and recommendations are aimed at the "you" this layer describes.
✅ Check for Understanding

Pause and think: A scholarship reviewer searches your name and pulls up two things: an old gaming-forum post you commented on three years ago, and a photo someone tagged you in at a school event. Which of the five signal layers do those come from — and which layer did you control?

03
Section 3 · Curated vs. Inferred

The identity you build vs. the identity others build for you

There are two parallel digital identities running for you at all times. There's the curated one you actively shape on purpose, and the inferred one that other people — and AI systems — assemble from the patterns of what you do. Strong digital citizens manage both.

🎨 Curated Identity
The version you build on purpose.
  • You chose every part of it
  • You can edit, delete, or rewrite
  • It's your "highlight reel"
  • Examples: profile bio, portfolio site, public posts you wrote on purpose
🧩 Inferred Identity
The version others assemble about you.
  • Built from patterns you didn't notice
  • Rarely fully erasable
  • It's your "data shadow"
  • Examples: ad-targeting categories, search snippets, screenshots of group chats, AI summaries of your activity
💭 Think About It

Summarizing Aristotle's view that character grows out of repeated action, the writer Will Durant put it this way: "We are what we repeatedly do." Online, your identity is the sum of your clicks — even the small ones. Every scroll, search, and reaction is a small vote toward who the algorithm believes you are. The pattern across a thousand small choices says more about you than any single post.

04
Section 4 · How Identity Builds Over Time

One click is a moment. A thousand clicks is a portrait.

A single post almost never defines a person. Patterns do. Here's how a digital identity is built one small action at a time — and why it's so hard to undo once a pattern has formed:

1
You make a choice
Every share, like, follow, search, comment, and screenshot is a small data event with your name attached. None of them feel like a "decision." All of them are.
2
The platform records it — usually forever
Most platforms keep a complete history of your activity, even after you "delete" something. Caches, screenshots, and AI training datasets often hold even more than the platform itself.
3
Future viewers see the pattern
A college admissions officer, future coach, or first employer doesn't see one post — they see the trend across years. Coherent patterns build trust. Contradictory patterns raise questions.
💡 Did You Know?

By the time the average student graduates high school, their devices have generated millions of data events tied to their accounts — search queries, location pings, message timestamps, scroll-pause durations, even how long their thumb hovered over a button. Each event is small. The pattern across all of them is enormous, and most of it cannot be deleted.

05
Section 5 · Traits of a Resilient Digital Identity

What makes an online identity that lasts well?

Plenty of online identities go viral. Far fewer age well. The ones that do share four traits — the same traits that make a real-world reputation strong:

🪞 Authentic
Matches the real you. Different platforms, same core values.
🎯 Intentional
You chose this version of yourself. You didn't drift into it.
🤝 Considerate
Respects the dignity of others, especially when no one is watching.
🛡️ Protected
Strong passwords, private settings on private accounts, two-factor on the big ones.
🌎 Real-World Connection

Hiring managers, scholarship committees, and college admissions officers report that they search candidates' names online for a majority of applicants. The version of you that lives on screen frequently arrives — and forms a first impression — before the real you ever walks through the door.

The digital identity habits you build in 8th grade are the same habits that professionals rely on for the rest of their careers.

📚 Key Vocabulary — Week 1

Digital identity
The picture other people and systems form of you based on what you do, post, and search online.
Persona
A deliberate version of yourself that you present in a specific space (school, gaming, art).
Digital footprint
The trail of data you leave behind every time you click, post, message, or search.
Curated content
Information you chose, edited, and published on purpose — your "highlight reel."
Inferred identity
What others (or algorithms) decide about you based on patterns in your activity.
Reputation
The cumulative opinion others form of you over time, from many small impressions.
Audience
Everyone — human or system — who can see what you post or signal online.
Permanence
The fact that most digital data is preserved indefinitely, even after "deletion."
🪞 Weekly Reflection
If a teacher you've never met searched your name online tonight, what story would they piece together?

Spend five honest minutes thinking about your real online presence — every account, every photo, every comment under your name. Then answer in 5–7 sentences:

  • Name one signal you control that you're proud of.
  • Name one signal you don't fully control (a tag, a screenshot, an old post) that you wish was different.
  • Name one change you'll make this week to bring your digital identity closer to the person you actually are.
