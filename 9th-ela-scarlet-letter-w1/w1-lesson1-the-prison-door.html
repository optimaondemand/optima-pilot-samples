The Writer’s World, 1804–1850

Tap each year. Numbers point to the sources below.

His first American ancestor, William Hathorne, was a longtime Salem magistrate known as a persecutor of Quakers.2 William’s son John Hathorne was a judge in the Salem witch trials of 1692.2, 3 Nathaniel later added the “w” to the family name.2, 3
Nathaniel Hathorne is born on July 4, 1804, in Salem, Massachusetts.1
He graduates from Bowdoin College in Maine, in the same class as the future poet Henry Wadsworth Longfellow.4
His first collection of stories, Twice-Told Tales, is published.5 Some of his tales are set in Puritan New England.15
In 1836 Ralph Waldo Emerson publishes Nature, calling for a philosophy of insight rather than tradition.6 In April 1841 Hawthorne joins Brook Farm, a community organized by the Transcendental Club, and leaves after a few months.6, 7
He marries Sophia Peabody on July 9, 1842, and moves to the Old Manse in Concord.7, 8 His neighbors include Emerson, Henry David Thoreau, and Bronson Alcott. He moves in their circle but never considers himself a Transcendentalist.7
Margaret Fuller, a writer in the Transcendentalist circle, publishes Woman in the Nineteenth Century.6
With help from friends in the Democratic Party, he becomes surveyor of the Salem Custom House.1, 8
On July 19–20, the first women’s rights convention meets at Seneca Falls, New York. Its Declaration of Sentiments demands women’s right to vote.9, 10 Revolutions spread across Europe.11
After the Whig Party takes the presidency, he loses the Custom House job in June.1, 8 Charles Upham, a Salem Whig who had published Lectures on Witchcraft in 1831, pressed for his removal.12, 13 His mother dies soon after.8 He turns to writing The Scarlet Letter.1
The Scarlet Letter is published in Boston. By March 30, 1850, Hawthorne is signing the preface to a second edition.14 The book opens with “The Custom-House,” a sketch whose narrator, a surveyor much like Hawthorne, claims to find a faded scarlet A and an old manuscript in the attic. The narrator borrows from Hawthorne’s life, but he is part of the fiction: the find is invented.14
