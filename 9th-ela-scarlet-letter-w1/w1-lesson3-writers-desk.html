Ten Words

contumely

noun

Insolent, scornful abuse in words or actions.

“Of an impulsive and passionate nature, she had fortified herself to encounter the stings and venomous stabs of public contumely, wreaking itself in every variety of insult · · ·” Ch. II

Root Latin contumelia, “insult, reproach”.

ignominy

noun

Public shame and disgrace.

“The very ideal of ignominy was embodied and made manifest in this contrivance of wood and iron.” Ch. II

Root Latin ignominia, from in- (“not”) + nomen (“name, reputation”).

pillory

noun

A wooden frame with holes for the head and hands, used to hold a person up to public view as punishment.

“It was, in short, the platform of the pillory; and above it rose the framework of that instrument of discipline · · ·” Ch. II

Root Old French pilori, a word of uncertain earlier origin.

iniquity

noun

Wickedness; a serious wrong.

“A blessing on the righteous Colony of the Massachusetts, where iniquity is dragged out into the sunshine!” Ch. II

Root Latin iniquitas, from in- (“not”) + aequus (“equal, fair”).

sumptuary

adjective

Limiting what people may spend, especially on clothing and luxuries.

“· · · a splendor in accordance with the taste of the age, but greatly beyond what was allowed by the sumptuary regulations of the colony.” Ch. II

Root Latin sumptuarius, from sumptus (“expense”).

malefactress

noun

A woman who has done wrong; a female wrongdoer.

“It would be greatly for the public behoof, if we women, being of mature age and church-members in good repute, should have the handling of such malefactresses as this Hester Prynne.” Ch. II

Root Latin male (“badly”) + facere (“to do”), with the feminine ending -ess.

penance

noun

A punishment or act of self-denial accepted to show sorrow for a wrong.

“It is probable that there was an idea of penance in this mode of occupation, and that she offered up a real sacrifice of enjoyment, in devoting so many hours to such rude handiwork.” Ch. V

Root Latin paenitentia, “repentance”.

alchemy

noun

The old art of trying to turn common metals into gold.

“I shall seek this man, as I have sought truth in books; as I have sought gold in alchemy.” Ch. IV

Root Arabic al-kimiya, through Medieval Latin alchimia.

ascetic

adjective

Severely plain; living with strict self-denial.

“Hester sought not to acquire anything beyond a subsistence, of the plainest and most ascetic description, for herself, and a simple abundance for her child.” Ch. V

Root Greek askētikos, from askein (“to exercise, to train”).

emblem

noun

An object or image that stands for an idea.

“An imp of evil, emblem and product of sin, she had no right among christened infants.” Ch. VI

Root Greek emblēma, “something inserted or inlaid”.
