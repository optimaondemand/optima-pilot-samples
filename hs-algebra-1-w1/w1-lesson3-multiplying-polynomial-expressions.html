Multiplying Polynomial Expressions

Focus

How do you make sure every term gets multiplied by every other term, and what do you do with the pieces afterward?

Objectives
  • Multiply a monomial by a binomial or trinomial using an area model and the distributive property.
  • Multiply two binomials and combine the like terms in the result.
  • Use the pattern for (x + a)(x + b) and the two special products: a squared binomial and a sum times a difference.
  • Multiply a binomial by a trinomial, with rational coefficients and degrees up to 3.

A

Distribute to Every Term

An area model is a picture of the distributive property: a(b + c) = ab + ac. The monomial multiplies every term inside the parentheses, one term at a time. When you multiply two terms, multiply the coefficients and add the exponents of the same variable. Each product keeps its own sign, and that sign becomes the operator in the answer.

ExampleMultiply −7x2(x2 − 4x − 11)
  1. Distribute −7x2 to all three terms: (−7x2 · x2) + (−7x2 · −4x) + (−7x2 · −11).
  2. First product: −7 times 1 is −7, and x2 · x2 = x4. That gives −7x4.
  3. Second product: −7 times −4 is 28, because a negative times a negative is positive, and x2 · x = x3. That gives +28x3.
  4. Third product: −7 times −11 is 77, and the variable part stays x2. That gives +77x2.
  5. Each sign becomes the operator, so the product is −7x4 + 28x3 + 77x2.

All the words in this lesson
Area modelA graphical picture of a multiplication that shows the distributive property. It is usually a rectangle: the length and width are the factors, and the area is the product. Algebra tiles let you build the same picture by hand.
BinomialAn algebraic expression with exactly two terms, separated by a plus or minus sign, such as 3x + 2.
CoefficientThe numerical factor in a term. In 4x, the coefficient is 4.
DegreeThe highest power of the variable in a polynomial. In 2x3 − 5x2 + x − 9, the degree is 3, because 3 is the largest exponent.
Distributive propertyMultiplication is distributive over addition and subtraction: a(b + c) = ab + ac.
EquivalentEquivalent expressions have the same value for every value of their variables. For instance, 2x + 3 and 3 + 2x are equivalent.
ExpressionA mathematical phrase with numbers, variables, operation symbols, and grouping symbols, such as 6x2 − 8x + 11.
MonomialAn algebraic expression with only one term: a number (5), a variable (x), or a number times a variable (5x).
TermA single number, a variable, or numbers and variables multiplied together. Terms are the building blocks of expressions and are separated by addition or subtraction signs. In 2x + 3, the terms are 2x and 3.
TrinomialAn algebraic expression with three terms, such as x2 + 5x + 4.

C

The Same Product by Distribution

Either method works for every product on this page, and you can choose whichever you like. With two binomials you will almost always have a pair of like terms in the middle. Combine them to get the fully simplified expression.

ExampleMultiply (−3x + 6)(5x − 8)
  1. Distribute each term of the first binomial across the second: −3x(5x − 8) + 6(5x − 8).
  2. Start with −3x: −3x · 5x = −15x2 and −3x · −8 = 24x.
  3. Then 6: 6 · 5x = 30x and 6 · −8 = −48.
  4. Write all four products: −15x2 + 24x + 30x − 48.
  5. The 24x and 30x are both positive, so add them to get 54x. The product is −15x2 + 54x − 48, the same answer the area model gave.

A pattern in the answer

Look at (x + 8)(x + 6) = x2 + 14x + 48. The middle coefficient, 14, is the sum of 8 and 6. The last term, 48, is their product. When both binomials start with a plain x, that works every time:

(x + a)(x + b) = x2 + (a + b)x + ab

It also works when a or b or both are subtracted. Treat the subtraction sign as a negative number. For (x − a)(x − b) the formula gives x2 + (−a − b)x + (−a)(−b), which is x2 − (a + b)x + ab. With minus signs in both parentheses, the middle term is negated but the last term stays positive, since a negative times a negative is positive.

E

Squaring a Binomial

Squared means multiplied by itself, so (3x − 5)2 = (3x − 5)(3x − 5). The same shortcut works whether the sign inside is plus or minus.

ExampleMultiply (3x − 5)2
  1. Write the square as a product: (3x − 5)(3x − 5).
  2. Fill the area model.
    ·3x−5
    3x9x2−15x
    −5−15x25
  3. The two middle terms, −15x and −15x, combine to −30x. The first term of the answer is the square of the first term, and the last term is the square of the last term.
  4. A shorter way: first term (3x)2 = 9x2; middle term 2(3x · −5) = 2(−15x) = −30x; last term (−5)2 = 25.
  5. The product is 9x2 − 30x + 25. This works for any squared binomial, with a plus or a minus inside: (ax + b)2 = (ax)2 + 2(a · b)x + b2.

F

A Sum Times a Difference

In (3x − 4)(3x + 4), the coefficients and constants are the same. The only difference between the parentheses is one subtraction and one addition. Watch for a pattern as you multiply.

ExampleMultiply (3x − 4)(3x + 4)
  1. Distribute each term of the first binomial: 3x · 3x + 3x · 4 + (−4) · 3x + (−4) · 4.
  2. That is 9x2 + 12x − 12x − 16. The first term is the square of the first term in the parentheses.
  3. The second and third terms are opposites, one positive and one negative, so they add to 0 and leave no middle term. The last term is the negative of 42.
  4. The product is 9x2 − 16. You can think of (ax + b)(ax − b) as (ax)2 − b2.

G

Every Term Meets Every Term

The biggest thing to remember: both terms of the binomial must multiply all three terms of the trinomial. That is six products. An area model helps because every cell holds one product, so you can see whether anything is missing. If you distribute instead, draw an arrow from each term of the binomial to each term of the trinomial as you go.

ExampleMultiply (−4x + 7)(x2 − 9x + 5)
  1. Two terms times three terms makes six products: two rows of three cells.
  2. Fill the area model one row at a time.
    ·x2−9x5
    −4x−4x336x2−20x
    77x2−63x35
  3. Write all six products in one line:

    −4x3 + 36x2 − 20x + 7x2 − 63x + 35

    Two sets of like terms need combining: the x2 terms and the x terms. This will almost always be true for a binomial times a trinomial.
  4. Combine: 36x2 + 7x2 = 43x2 and −20x − 63x = −83x.
  5. The product is −4x3 + 43x2 − 83x + 35. Distributing with arrows gives the same six products.
