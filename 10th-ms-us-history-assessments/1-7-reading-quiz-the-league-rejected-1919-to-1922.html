WEEK 19 · READING QUIZ
Reading Quiz: The League Rejected, 1919 to 1922
6 questions · 6 points · one attempt
Before you start

Answer each question using what you learned in Lesson 1.2. You may look back at the lesson, but the answers are not stated word for word. You will need to put facts together.

QUESTION 1

Wilson tied the League of Nations to the Treaty of Versailles in a single package. Why did this choice make the League harder to pass?

A. It forced Germany to join the League before the Senate voted.
B. It required American voters to approve the treaty in a nationwide vote.
C. A senator who opposed the treaty's terms could not vote for the League without also voting for the treaty.
D. It gave Britain and France the power to cancel the Senate's vote.
QUESTION 2

Suppose the League's Council had asked the United States to send troops to defend a member nation that had been invaded. Which constitutional rule made Senator Lodge uneasy about that kind of promise?

A. Only Congress has the power to declare war.
B. Treaties must be approved by every state legislature.
C. The Supreme Court must approve any use of troops abroad.
D. The House of Representatives must approve each treaty by a two-thirds vote.
QUESTION 3

On November 19, 1919, the irreconcilables voted no on the treaty with Lodge's reservations and also no on the treaty without them. What does this tell you about them?

A. They were loyal to Wilson and followed his instructions.
B. They wanted the treaty to pass with a different set of reservations.
C. They had not read either version of the treaty.
D. They opposed the League itself, not just its wording.
QUESTION 4

The lesson says the outcome "was not written in advance." Which statement best supports that claim?

A. Most senators had made up their minds before Wilson returned from Paris.
B. A more flexible Wilson, or a healthier one, might have made a deal that changed the vote.
C. The Constitution gave the Senate no way to approve the treaty.
D. The American people were fully united against the League.
QUESTION 5

Warren G. Harding won more than sixty percent of the vote in 1920. Which reading of that result fits the lesson best?

A. Voters approved of the Treaty of Versailles and wanted it strengthened.
B. Voters wanted the United States to join the League at once.
C. Voters cared only about the economy and gave little thought to the war.
D. Many voters were tired of crusades, and the League had become tied to Wilson's name.
QUESTION 6

Which detail from 1921 and 1922 best supports the claim that rejecting the League did not mean the United States withdrew completely from world affairs?

A. Secretary of State Hughes proposed limits on battleships, and the Senate approved a naval treaty among the great powers.
B. Wilson took a speaking tour of the West by train.
C. Harding promised a "return to normalcy."
D. Lodge drew up a list of reservations to the treaty.
