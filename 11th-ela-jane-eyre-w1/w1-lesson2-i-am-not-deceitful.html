Focus

A stranger arrives, and the adults describe a ten-year-old as if she were not in the room. Who gets to name Jane, and what does she do with the names?

Objectives
  • Track the words adults use to name Jane, and what each word is meant to do.
  • Tell who owns which words: Mr. Brocklehurst, Mrs. Reed, Jane, and the narrator.
  • Read Jane’s reply to Mrs. Reed as a turn, where she takes a word and hands it back.
  • Weigh the narrator’s judgment of the victory, using the images of fire and wine.
  • Support a reading with Brontë’s exact words, cited by chapter.

The Turn

“I am not deceitful: if I were, I should say I loved you” (Ch. IV). What does Jane do with the word “deceitful”?

Mrs. Reed asks “What more have you to say?” in the tone the narrator describes: “rather in the tone in which a person might address an opponent of adult age than such as is ordinarily used to a child” (Ch. IV). Why does she speak that way?

Jane cries “Deceit is not my fault!” Mrs. Reed answers “children must be corrected for their faults” (Ch. IV). What does the answer do?
