The Writer’s World, 1816–1847

Tap each year. Numbers point to the sources below.

Charlotte Brontë is born on April 21, 1816, at Thornton, Yorkshire, in the north of England, the daughter of a clergyman, Patrick Brontë.1, 5
In February 1820 the family moves to the parsonage at Haworth, a village on the edge of the Yorkshire moors.1 Her mother dies in September 1821.1
Her father sends his eldest daughters, Maria and Elizabeth, to the Clergy Daughters’ School at Cowan Bridge, and Charlotte and Emily soon follow.1, 5 In 1825 Maria and Elizabeth fall ill and come home to die: Maria on May 6, Elizabeth on June 15.1, 5
In January 1831 she goes to school at Roe Head, near Huddersfield.1, 5 From 1835 to 1838 she returns there as a teacher, earning money for her family.5
A new Poor Law means poor people can get help only by leaving their homes for a workhouse, where conditions are deliberately harsh so that only the desperate will ask.4
She takes a post as a governess, teaching another family’s children in their home.1
In February 1842 she and Emily enter Madame Heger’s pensionnat, a girls’ school in Brussels, to study languages. In 1843 she goes back to Brussels alone.1, 5
Charlotte, Emily, and Anne publish Poems under the names Currer, Ellis, and Acton Bell, paying for it themselves.5 Charlotte later explains: “Averse to personal publicity, we veiled our own names”; they feared that women writers were “liable to be looked on with prejudice.” Of the book itself she writes, “it is scarcely known.”3
Smith, Elder & Co. of London publish Jane Eyre: An Autobiography under the name Currer Bell. On October 19, 1847, Brontë writes to thank them: “The six copies of ‘Jane Eyre’ reached me this morning.”2 Jane is invented. The subtitle is part of the fiction: the book asks to be read as a real woman’s account of her own life. Brontë sets the story in the north of England and never gives a year.6
She dies at Haworth on March 31, 1855, aged 38.2, 5

“No; you are less than a servant, for you do nothing for your keep.” Ch. II
