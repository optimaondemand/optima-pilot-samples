The Bargain
Jessie L. Weston, translator · 1912 · Sir Gawain and the Green Knight, Book II

'First,' quoth the host, 'we'll make a forward fair and free, Whate'er in wood I win the profit thine shall be, What cheer thou shalt achieve, shalt give me, 'gainst my gain; Now swear me here with truth to keep this 'twixt us twain Whate'er our hap may be, or good or ill befall. — ' ' By God,' quoth good Gawain, ' I grant ye this withal, An such play pleaseth you, forsooth it pleaseth me — ' 'Now, bring the beverage here, the bargain set shall be.'

🔤 Words: The Green Lace
The other five of this week’s ten words. Tap a card to hear it.
courtesy
Polite, kind, respectful behavior.
“…Gawain turned them all with jest and courtesy, as a true knight should who speaks with the lady of his host.” Green, Sir Gawain and the Green Knight
favor
A token, such as a ribbon or lace, that a lady gives a knight to wear for her. Green spells it favour.
“…I cannot be your knight and wear your favour.” Green, Sir Gawain and the Green Knight
temptation
A strong pull to do something you know you should not.
“This proved too great a temptation for Gawain…” Green, Sir Gawain and the Green Knight
covetousness
Wanting to have or keep something too much; greed.
“For cowardice and covetousness I betrayed my oath of knighthood.” Green, Sir Gawain and the Green Knight
penance
Something hard you accept to make up for a wrong.
“You have borne your penance, and are quite absolved and forgiven.” Green, Sir Gawain and the Green Knight

Also in the chapter: a girdle is a belt, and a lace is a cord.
