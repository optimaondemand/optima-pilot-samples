READING QUIZ
Reading Quiz: Dred Scott to the 14th Amendment: Who Counts as a Citizen
5 questions · 5 points · 2 attempts
Before you start

Five questions on how Americans have answered the question of who counts as a citizen.

QUESTION 1
The 14th Amendment never names Dred Scott. What lets you conclude that it overturned Taney's view of citizenship?
A. It was ratified after the Civil War by a Congress that disliked the Court.
B. It makes birth and naturalization the two roads to citizenship and names no race, so no one can be shut out as Taney said.
C. It banned slavery in the territories where Scott had lived.
D. It gave each state the power to decide who its citizens are.
QUESTION 2
Wong Kim Ark's parents could not be naturalized, yet the Supreme Court said he was a citizen. Which reasoning best explains the decision?
A. His parents became citizens while he was away in China.
B. The Chinese Exclusion Act had been struck down.
C. His birth on American soil made him a citizen, whatever his parents' status.
D. The Court decided that the 14th Amendment did not apply to children.
QUESTION 3
The lesson says a Court with more restraint "might have spared the country" the decision in Dred Scott. What does that suggest about what the Court did?
A. It answered the biggest question it could when it could have ruled more narrowly.
B. It ruled on a question that the Constitution forbids courts to answer.
C. It followed a plan that every justice had agreed on from the start.
D. It ruled for Dred Scott but gave no reasons.
QUESTION 4
People born in American Samoa are U.S. nationals, not automatic citizens. What does this show about citizenship?
A. Anyone born under the American flag is a citizen with no exceptions.
B. Nationals owe no loyalty to the United States.
C. Puerto Rico and American Samoa were treated in exactly the same way.
D. Citizenship in the territories is still set by Congress and the courts.
QUESTION 5
The Chinese Exclusion Act was passed after the 14th Amendment. What does the pairing of the two show?
A. A country can write a generous rule for birth and still be stingy about who may join.
B. The 14th Amendment was repealed when the Act passed.
C. Congress has no power over immigration.
D. Chinese immigrants were granted naturalization soon after.
