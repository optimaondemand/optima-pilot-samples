01  Introduction
 
Open with this: This is from ______, ______ to ______. Just before this, ______. I chose it because ______.
Other wording is permitted if it carries the same four facts.
02  The reading
 
SOURCEJane Eyre, chapters 1 to 27, any passage of 450 to 650 words. Use your own copy of the novel.
LENGTH450 to 650 words. Check the word count. Do not count by hand.
TEXTRead it as written. No summarising, no skipping, no paraphrase.
PREPTwo rehearsals. The first finds the words that trip you. The second decides where the sentences breathe.
NAMESUnfamiliar names are attempted out loud, not skipped.
Passages that carry aloud
›Chapter 2: the red-room, from the door closing on Jane to the fit that ends the chapter.
›Chapter 4: Jane’s answer to Mrs. Reed, and the heath-fire and wine that follow it.
›Chapter 9: Helen Burns’s last night, from Jane’s climb into the bed to the morning.
›Chapter 12: Jane on the third storey, restless, on what women feel.
›Chapter 23: the orchard, from the nightingale to Jane’s refusal to be caught.
›Chapter 27: Jane’s answer to Rochester on the morning after the wedding.
Name the chapter in your introduction.
03  The conversation
 
Keep recording. Complete one of the three. They carry equal weight.
AOff the cuff1 to 2 min
Answer any two.
1What does this show about the character that you did not know before?
2Which one word is doing the most work?
3What did reading it aloud show you that reading it silently did not?
4What question does it leave you with?
BThe case1 to 2 min
Argue something debatable about the passage. Take the side most people will not.
1Name the arguable thing.
2State your position in one sentence. If nobody could disagree, it is not an argument yet.
3Point at the words. You just read them.
4Answer the strongest objection.
COne line, rewritten1 to 2 min
Rewind to one moment and change it.
1Name the exact sentence you are changing.
2Say what happens instead.
3Say what the author’s version does that yours does not. Then say whether you still prefer yours.
04  Sign-off
 
One sentence. Name the show, and let the passage have the last word if you like: a phrase from it, read again.

Worth knowing: AI and your reading
 
An AI tool can misquote a passage or invent lines. Read from the novel itself, not from a chatbot. The reading and the response have to be yours. Using AI is optional.
