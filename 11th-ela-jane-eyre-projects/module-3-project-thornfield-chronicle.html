Module 3 Project: The Thornfield Chronicle
English 3 · Module 3 · Jane Eyre
50 pointsInformative feature, 900 to 1,200 wordsDue in Week 9
What you make
 
You write one informative feature for the Thornfield Chronicle, a literary-cultural periodical, for a general reader who has not read the novel. Your feature explains one part of Jane’s world.
Focus question
 
If a reader has never opened Jane Eyre and never will, what one part of her world do you want them to understand, and can you make them understand it in under 1,200 words?
Choose one angle
 
Angle
AThe real position of a Victorian governess
BThe Gothic mode, and why Brontë uses it
CInheritance, marriage law, and money in the novel
DThe shape of a coming-of-age story, and how Jane Eyre fits and bends it
Your reading is yours to make. Any of the four angles, and any argument the evidence supports, can earn full marks. Name your general reader before you begin: a specific person, not “everyone.”
What the feature includes
 
1Headline, standfirst, and byline. The headline names the topic; the standfirst is one or two sentences that tell a stranger why to read on.
2A lead that hooks a stranger. The first lines have to earn the next paragraph for someone who has never met Jane.
3Headed sections. Each holds one idea, and the headings share a grammatical form.
4At least one pull-quote from the novel, exact and cited by chapter.
5A sidebar: a short box with a fact, a definition, or a timeline that a reader can use.
6At least two credible sources, one from JStor, woven into the explanation and cited in MLA, with a source list.
Steps
 
1Choose your angle and your general reader. Write the sentence you would open with.
2Find at least two credible sources, one from JStor. Note what each adds that the novel alone does not.
3Plan the page: headline, standfirst, lead, headed sections, pull-quote, sidebar, sources.
4Draft 900 to 1,200 words. Every claim about the novel is cited by chapter. Every fact about the real world has a source behind it.
5Revise: cut, reorder, sharpen. Hand your lead to someone who has not read the novel and see where they stall.
6Edit and verify: concision, consistent headings and tense, agreement, quotation punctuation, and a full MLA check. Confirm every quotation and citation.

Worth knowing: AI and your feature
 
An AI tool can flag an undefined term or a claim that needs a source. It can also state dates, wages, and laws with confidence and get them wrong, name sources that do not exist, and misquote the novel. Verify every source on JStor before you cite it, and every quotation against the text. The sentences of your feature are yours to write. Using AI is optional. If you use it, keep a short log of what you asked, what it said, and what you kept, and submit the log with your feature.
