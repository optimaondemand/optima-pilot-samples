Graded Recitation: A Victorian Poem
English 3 · Module 3 · Jane Eyre · Week 6
20 pointsFrom memory, eyes coveredVideo or live
What you do
 
Recite a Victorian-era poem from memory, with your eyes covered. Choose one poem from the list below, or its marked span.
The rule
 
Eyes covered. Wear a blindfold, close your eyes, or turn away from any page or screen. No reading.

Two ways to turn it in
 
Option A, a recording. Record yourself on any device, eyes covered, with clear audio of your voice. Submit the video or audio file, or paste a link, with a caption or transcript of the exact lines you recited, the poem’s title, and the chapter for options 1 and 2. The transcript lets your teacher check accuracy without playing the whole file.
Option B, live. Your teacher may instead schedule a time to hear you recite, also with your eyes closed. If so, you do not submit a recording.
Steps
 
1Choose one poem and read it aloud several times.
2Learn it by heart. Let the line breaks tell you where to breathe, and treat the meter as a pulse, not a hammer.
3Decide where the poem turns: the moment the sound, the speaker, or the argument changes. Let your voice mark it.
4Recite it with your eyes covered, on a recording or live.
5Add the caption or transcript, and submit.
Done when
 
☐I recited from memory, eyes covered, faithful to the words and the line order
☐My pace, pauses, and stress follow the sense of the poem, not only the line ends
☐My voice shows where the poem turns
☐My transcript names the poem and, for options 1 and 2, the chapter

Worth knowing: AI and your recitation
 
An AI tool can misremember a poem, skip lines, or mix two poems together. Copy your transcript from the poem’s own text, not from a chatbot, and check it line by line. The memorizing and the voice have to be yours. Using AI is optional.
