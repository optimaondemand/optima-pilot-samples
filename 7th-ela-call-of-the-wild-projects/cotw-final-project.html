The Call of the Wild: Your Final Project
 

Argumentative essay (about 500 to 700 words) + transformation visual + two-worlds scene + spoken explanation + reflection

Focus question
 
Does Buck's transformation set him free, or destroy who he was?
What you are making
 

Buck begins as the king of a warm, comfortable home and ends as a creature of the wild, running with the wolves. Readers disagree about what that change means. Some say the wild sets Buck free to become what he truly is. Others say it destroys the loyal, loving dog he once was.

Your project answers the focus question two ways. First, an argumentative essay: you have practiced it in Quick Writes 2, 3, 4, and 5 (a thesis, evidence with explanation, a counterclaim, and an outline with an introduction), and the Week 6 lessons take you from plan to polished essay. Second, a creative piece: a visual and a two-worlds scene that let a viewer see and hear the change. The Week 6 lessons coach both, and your Week 6 PDF holds that work.

The essay
 

Write an argumentative essay that answers the focus question. Take a clear position, state it as a thesis, support it with evidence from the book and explain that evidence, raise the strongest argument against your view and answer it, then close with a conclusion. Build it from your Quick Writes 2 to 5 and your Transformation Log, saved in your lesson PDFs in your OneDrive course folder. About 500 to 700 words, about 70 minutes of writing time, spread out.

1
Write your thesis.
Decide your position on the focus question: does the change set Buck free, or destroy who he was? Write one clear sentence that states your answer. This is your thesis. Put it at the end of a short opening paragraph that sets up the question.
2
Give your evidence and explain it.
Use two or three specific details or short quotations from the book that support your thesis. Cite each one in MLA format: London's last name plus the chapter, for example (London, Ch. 3). If you already name London in your sentence, just give the chapter. After each one, write a sentence or two that shows how it proves your point. Do not just drop the quotation; explain what it means. Pull these from your Quick Writes and your Transformation Log.
3
Raise a counterclaim and answer it.
Name the strongest argument someone on the other side would make. For example, if you argue the wild sets Buck free, a reader might point to how much he loves John Thornton. State that fairly in a sentence or two, then explain why your position still holds.
4
Write a conclusion.
End with two or three sentences that restate your thesis in fresh words and say what London wants readers to understand about instinct, survival, and the pull between civilization and the wild.
5
Add your Works Cited entry.
At the end of your document, add one entry for the novel: London, Jack. The Call of the Wild. 1903.
6
Be honest about your reasoning.
If a detail only partly fits, say so. Taking the other side seriously in your counterclaim makes your argument stronger, not weaker.

The creative piece
 

Buck starts as a pampered house dog in the sunny Santa Clara Valley and ends as a creature of the wild. Show that transformation two ways: a visual you create, and a two-worlds scene that sets his civilized world and the wild side by side.

A · Your transformation visual (choose one)

Create one visual that shows Buck's transformation from the tame house dog to the creature of the wild. Pick the format you like best:

›A "before and after" art piece, drawn by hand or made with any digital tool, showing Buck at Judge Miller's place beside Buck in the wild.
›A comic strip of three to six panels that shows the key moments of Buck's change.
›A map of Buck's journey from the Santa Clara Valley to the Northland, marking the places where he changes the most.

Whichever you choose, the transformation must be obvious, and your visual must point at the theme: that Buck is pulled between civilization and the wild, and the wild wins.

B · Two Worlds, One Frame
1
Make one scene with two sides: a single PowerPoint slide, a drawing, a collage, or a photograph of a small model you build.
2
On one side, Buck's civilized world: warm, comfortable objects that suggest the big house and easy living. On the other side, the wild: cold, open objects that suggest the Northland.
3
Between them, a path of three to five objects or moments that show the journey.
4
Add two labels, one per side, each one sentence naming who Buck is in that world.
5
Save it as an image or PDF named LastName_CotW_TwoWorlds.
6
Record a spoken explanation, 30 to 60 seconds: walk a viewer from Buck's civilized world to the wild, in order, and name the one key point your scene makes about the pull between civilization and the wild. Record it with PowerPoint's Record button, Canvas media recording, or a phone or laptop. This spoken part is required and graded for everyone.
C · Your reflection (200 to 250 words)

Write a reflection of 200 to 250 words in Word. Explain what you learned about Buck's transformation from building this contrast. What did placing the two worlds side by side show you that reading alone did not? How do the two sides help you understand the pull between civilization and the wild, and your answer to the focus question? Name it LastName_CotW_ProjectReflection and save it to your OneDrive course folder.
