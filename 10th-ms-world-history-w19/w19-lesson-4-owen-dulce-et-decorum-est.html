Chapter 1

About This Poem

Wilfred Owen was a young British officer who fought on the Western Front. He wrote this poem in 1917 and 1918, and he was killed in battle on November 4, 1918, one week before the war ended. This poem was published after his death.

The Latin words at the end come from the Roman poet Horace. They mean, “It is sweet and fitting to die for one’s country.” Owen calls them “the old Lie.”

Several terms may be new. A “flare” is a rocket that lights up the battlefield at night. “Gas-shells” are artillery shells filled with poison gas. “Lime” is a chemical that burns skin.

A poem is a source like any other. Ask what the author saw, whom he is speaking to, and what he wants that person to feel and do.

The March and the Gas

Chapter 2

Stanzas 1 and 2, in Plain English

Plain version, written for this page. These are not Owen's words.

The men were bent double, like old beggars under sacks, knock-kneed, coughing like hags, and they cursed as they trudged through sludge. They turned their backs on the haunting flares and began to trudge toward their distant rest. Men marched asleep. Many had lost their boots but limped on, shod in blood. All went lame; all blind; drunk with fatigue; deaf even to the hoots of gas-shells dropping softly behind.

Then came the cry: Gas! GAS! Quick, boys! There was a wild fumbling as the men fitted the clumsy helmets on just in time. But someone was still yelling and stumbling, floundering like a man in fire or lime. Through the misty panes of the helmet, in a thick green light, as under a green sea, the speaker saw him drowning.

Chapter 3

Stanzas 1 and 2, in the Original

The original: stanzas 1 and 2

Bent double, like old beggars under sacks,
Knock-kneed, coughing like hags, we cursed through sludge,
Till on the haunting flares we turned our backs
And towards our distant rest began to trudge.
Men marched asleep. Many had lost their boots
But limped on, blood-shod. All went lame; all blind;
Drunk with fatigue; deaf even to the hoots
Of gas-shells dropping softly behind.

Gas! GAS! Quick, boys!--An ecstasy of fumbling
Fitting the clumsy helmets just in time,
But someone still was yelling out and stumbling
And flound’ring like a man in fire or lime.--
Dim, through the misty panes and thick green light,
As under a green sea, I saw him drowning.

The Dream and the Old Lie

Chapter 4

Stanzas 3 and 4, in Plain English

Plain version, written for this page. These are not Owen's words.

In all the speaker's dreams, before his helpless sight, the man plunges at him, guttering, choking, drowning.

The speaker says to his friend: if in some smothering dreams you too could pace behind the wagon that we flung him in, and watch the white eyes writhing in his face, his hanging face, like a devil's sick of sin; if you could hear, at every jolt, the blood come gargling from the froth-corrupted lungs, bitter as the cud of vile, incurable sores on innocent tongues, then you would not tell with such high zest, to children ardent for some desperate glory, the old Lie: “It is sweet and fitting to die for one's country.”

Chapter 5

Stanzas 3 and 4, in the Original

The original: stanzas 3 and 4

In all my dreams before my helpless sight
He plunges at me, guttering, choking, drowning.

If in some smothering dreams you too could pace
Behind the wagon that we flung him in,
And watch the white eyes writhing in his face,
His hanging face, like a devil’s sick of sin,
If you could hear, at every jolt, the blood
Come gargling from the froth-corrupted lungs,
Bitter as the cud
Of vile, incurable sores on innocent tongues,--
My friend, you would not tell with such high zest
To children ardent for some desperate glory,
The old Lie: Dulce et decorum est
Pro patria mori.

Chapter 6

A Note for You

From the page

A poem like this tells you what one man saw and how he wanted others to see it. It is powerful evidence of the horror of one kind of attack. But Owen also wrote to change minds, and many soldiers who survived the war felt differently about their service. Keep both thoughts in view as you read.
