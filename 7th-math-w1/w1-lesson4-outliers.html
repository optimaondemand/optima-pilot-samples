Effects of Outliers on Measures

Focus

What does one value that is far from all the others do to the mean, the median, the range, and the IQR?

Objectives
  • Explain why it matters to understand how changes in data affect the measures in real situations.
  • Tell what an outlier is and how to spot one in a data set.
  • Describe how an outlier affects the mean, median, mode, range, and IQR.

What Is an Outlier?

Outlier

A data point that is very different from the others. It stands out because it is much higher or much lower than the rest of the data.

An outlier is not always a mistake. Sometimes it is a true extreme that gives useful insight into the data being studied.

Where outliers come from
Measurement errorsData entry errorsNatural variation

Effects on Each Measure

Meanbig effect

An outlier can change the mean a lot, pulling it toward the outlier’s value. The mean becomes a less reliable measure of the center.

Mediansmall effect

The median is the middle value of the ordered data, so it is not affected by how extreme any value is.

Rangebig effect

The range uses the highest and lowest values, so it is very sensitive to outliers.

IQRsmall effect

The IQR looks at the middle 50% of the data and ignores the extremes. It is a reliable measure of spread even with outliers.

Mode

The mode changes only if the count of the most common value is affected.

Most affected by an outlier: the mean and the range.
Least affected by an outlier: the median and the IQR.

Always think about the situation behind the data, and look for outliers, before you report what the data shows.

Vocab
DataInformation collected as numbers or observations, often used for analysis. Example: heights of students in a class.
Interquartile range (IQR)How spread out the middle half of a data set is: Q3 minus Q1. If Q1 is 25 and Q3 is 75, the IQR is 75 − 25 = 50.
MeanThe average: add all the numbers, then divide by how many there are. The mean of 2, 4, and 6 is (2 + 4 + 6) ÷ 3 = 4.
Measures of centerValues that represent the middle of a data set: the mean, median, and mode.
Measures of variationValues that describe how spread out a data set is: the range and interquartile range.
MedianThe middle value when the numbers are in order. With an even number of values, the average of the two middle numbers. The median of 3, 5, 7, 9 is 6.
ModeThe number that appears most often. A data set can have more than one mode or none. The mode of 2, 2, 3, 5, 7 is 2.
OutlierA value much higher or lower than the others. It can affect the mean and other measures. In 3, 4, 5, 45, 6, the outlier is 45.
QuartilesValues that divide a data set into four equal parts. Q1 is the median of the lower half; Q3 is the median of the upper half.
RangeThe difference between the highest and lowest values. The range of 3, 7, 10 is 10 − 3 = 7.
Skewed dataData that is not symmetric. Skewed left has a longer tail on the left; skewed right, on the right. It can affect the mean and median.

Example 2A meteorologist records the daily high temperatures for five days, in °F: 70, 72, 73, 75, 76. Then a heatwave hits, with a high of 105. Find the effect of the outlier.
Your turn

What is the new mean?

What is the new range?

  1. Original: mean = 366 ÷ 5 = 73.2  ·  median = 73  ·  range = 76 − 70 = 6  ·  IQR = 75 − 72 = 3
  2. With the outlier: 70, 72, 73, 75, 76, 105
  3. New mean = 471 ÷ 6 = 78.5, significantly higher.
  4. New median = (73 + 75) ÷ 2 = 74, slightly higher.
  5. New range = 105 − 70 = 35, significantly higher.
  6. New IQR: lower half 70, 72, 73 gives Q1 = 72. Upper half 75, 76, 105 gives Q3 = 76. IQR = 76 − 72 = 4, a small change.
  7. Interpret: the heatwave day, as an outlier, raises the mean and the range. An extreme temperature can change how we see these days of weather.
