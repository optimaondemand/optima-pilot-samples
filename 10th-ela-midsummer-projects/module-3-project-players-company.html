Module 3 Project: The Players’ Company
English 2 · Module 3 · A Midsummer Night’s Dream · Week 9
50 pointsProgram PDF + recording + Project PDFDue in Week 9
What you make
 
You direct one scene of 30 to 80 lines from the play and publish it twice: a Director’s Program for the theatergoer who reads before the curtain rises, and a Director’s Call for the cast.
Focus question
 
How does Shakespeare build layers of meaning and mood in one scene, and which layer can a production bring out for a theatergoer?
The two parts
 
The Director’s Program. A publication-ready PDF built in Word or PowerPoint: a Director’s Essay of 700 to 1,000 words on how Shakespeare builds layers of meaning and mood in your scene and how your production brings one layer out; a Design Page; a cast list with one-line character notes; and one cited passage.
The Director’s Call. A recording of 2 to 3 minutes, audio or video, in which you give the cast your instructions for the key moment of the scene: blocking, delivery, and pause, in clear sequence.
Your reading is yours to make. Any scene of 30 to 80 lines from the play, and any interpretation the text supports, can earn full marks. Two directors who read the same scene differently can both earn 50 out of 50. The score rewards the quality of the thinking and the publication.
Steps
 
1Choose one scene of 30 to 80 lines from the play. Read it through before you plan anything.
2Write the Director’s Essay: 700 to 1,000 words under headings, with a thesis, quoted lines cited by act and scene and explained, a comparison of the text with a staged interpretation, and the one layer your production brings out.
3Build the program in Word or PowerPoint: a cover, the essay, a Design Page (a setting and a costume or color concept, each with a captioned image or drawing, a credit, and a one-line rationale), a cast list with one-line character notes, and one cited passage.
4Export the program to PDF.
5Record the Director’s Call: 2 to 3 minutes, audio or video, giving the cast your instructions for the key moment in order, with a plan for pace and pause.
6Submit the program, the recording, and your Project PDF on this assignment.
Done when
 
☐The essay explains how the scene builds layers of meaning and mood, and names the one layer the production brings out
☐The essay compares the text with a staged interpretation and names what each emphasizes and what each leaves out
☐Quoted lines are accurate, cited by act and scene, and explained through their wording
☐The program is organized under clear headings, with a design page, a cast list, and one cited passage, and every image has a caption, a credit, and alt text
☐The recording is 2 to 3 minutes, with clear instructions in order and a plan for pace and pause

Your recorded call
 
Use whatever device you have: a phone, a tablet, a computer webcam, or a school recording tool. Audio or video is accepted. Aim for 2 to 3 minutes. If your tool saves a file, upload it to this assignment. If it gives you a link, paste the link in the Canvas text-entry box.
Worth knowing: AI and your program
 
An AI tool can write a director’s note in seconds. It can also misstate the play, invent lines, and describe scenes that are not in the text, so check every claim against the play. An AI concept image may appear on the design page only if it is labeled as AI generated and followed by a sentence critiquing what it got wrong. If you ask an AI tool for help with ideas, keep a short note of what you asked and what you checked yourself, and include it with your program. Using AI is optional.
