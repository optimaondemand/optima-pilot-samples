Secants and Tangents

Focus

Lines from one point outside a circle trade equal lengths for equal products. Which pieces do you multiply, and when are two segments simply equal?

Objectives
  • Describe how the lengths of tangents, secants, and chords in a circle are related.
  • Use the Two Tangents, Two Secants, and Secant-Tangent theorems to find unknown lengths.
  • Write and solve an equation when segment lengths are expressions with x.
  • Apply the segment relationships to a real situation with a circle.

Tangents From One Point

Two Tangents Theorem

If two tangents are drawn from the same external point to a circle, the two tangent segments are always congruent. A tangent segment runs from the outside point to the point of tangency.

PO = NO

ExampleCircle M has tangents PO and NO. PO = 5x − 4 and NO = 3x + 6. Find x and the lengths of PO and NO.
  1. Two tangents drawn from the same external point are congruent, so PO = NO.
  2. Substitute the expressions: 5x − 4 = 3x + 6.
  3. Subtract 3x from both sides: 2x − 4 = 6. Add 4: 2x = 10. Divide by 2: x = 5.
  4. Substitute x = 5 into either expression: PO = 5(5) − 4 = 25 − 4 = 21. Since the segments are congruent, NO = 21 too.
  5. The value of x is 5, and PO and NO are each 21 units.

All the words in this lesson
CenterThe point inside a circle that is equidistant from all points on the circle.
ChordA line segment with both endpoints on the circle.
CircleThe set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a given point called the center.
External segmentThe portion of a secant segment that lies outside the circle, extending from an external point to the circle’s boundary.
Internal segmentThe portion of a secant segment that lies inside the circle, extending from the point where the secant enters the circle to the second intersection point.
Point of tangencyThe point at which a tangent line touches the circle.
SecantA line that intersects a circle at two points.
SegmentA part of a line that has two endpoints and a measurable length. In a circle, segments can include chords, secant segments, radii, and diameters.
TangentA line that touches a circle at exactly one point without crossing it.

Outside Piece Times Whole Secant

External segmentThe part of a secant outside the circle, from the outside point to the circle. In the figure: x and y.
Internal segmentThe part of a secant inside the circle, from where it enters to where it leaves. In the figure: w and z.
Two Secants Theorem

If two secant segments are drawn from the same external point, the product of the external segment and the total length of one secant equals the product of the external segment and the total length of the other secant.

x(x + w) = y(y + z)

ExampleCircle G has secants KJ and IJ that meet at point J. KL = 2x + 8, LJ = 4, HJ = 6, and IH = 3x − 2. Find x and the length of KJ.
  1. Both secants start at the outside point J, so use the Two Secants Theorem: outside piece times whole secant is the same for both.
  2. One secant has outside piece JL = 4 and whole length JK = 4 + 2x + 8. The other has outside piece JH = 6 and whole length JI = 6 + 3x − 2.
  3. Set the two products equal: 4(4 + 2x + 8) = 6(6 + 3x − 2).
  4. Combine inside the parentheses: 4(2x + 12) = 6(3x + 4). Distribute: 8x + 48 = 18x + 24.
  5. Subtract 8x and 24 from both sides: 24 = 10x. Divide by 10: x = 2.4.
  6. The question asks for the full length of KJ, so use the whole secant: 4 + 2(2.4) + 8 = 4 + 4.8 + 8 = 16.8.
  7. Check the products: 4(16.8) = 67.2 and 6(6 + 7.2 − 2) = 6(11.2) = 67.2. They match.
  8. The value of x is 2.4, and KJ is 16.8.

The Tangent Squared

Secant-Tangent Theorem

If a tangent and a secant are drawn from the same external point, the square of the tangent’s length equals the product of the external segment and the total length of the secant.

g2 = f(f + e)

ExampleCircle M has secant OP and tangent ON meeting at point O. OQ = 4, QP = 8, and ON = x. Find the length of ON.
  1. A tangent and a secant leave the same outside point, so use the Secant-Tangent Theorem: ON2 = OQ(OQ + QP).
  2. Substitute the values: x2 = 4(4 + 8). The whole secant OP is 4 + 8 = 12.
  3. Multiply: x2 = 4(12) = 48.
  4. Take the square root of both sides: x = √48.
  5. Simplify. Since 48 = 16 · 3 and 16 is a perfect square, √48 = 4√3. Either form is acceptable.
  6. Round to a decimal only when a question asks for an approximation.
  7. The length of ON is √48 = 4√3.
