How it is graded
The story
World-buildingThe reader discovers the world through the senses, the way the traveler does.
Arc and point of viewA clear arc with a change that matters; one steady point of view.
ImagesFresh, exact images and comparisons that make the strange world vivid.
Clean sentencesControlled, varied sentences; errors are rare.
The speech
VoiceLong, gathered sentences and restrained detail; wonder and judgment together.
Your ownEvery line is yours, and the speech fits the character who gives it.
DeliveryFrom memory, clearly paced and easy to hear; it sounds like speech, not reading.

📌 Watch how Jonah brainstormed
Jonah’s brainstorm
Jonah · 8th Grade Scholar

Traveler: Tess, a mapmaker, sent alone to survey an ice moon. She wants to finish the map and go home. Her lander’s radio dies on the first day.

Place: An ice moon. One long ridge, a black sky, and one dark edge of it.

Senses: Sound: snow that squeaks instead of crunching. Smell: warm plastic and her own breath in the helmet. Sight: a slow blue glow far under the ice. Touch: cold pressing through her gloves. Taste: metal, from the air tank.

Being: Forty tall frost-pillars on the ridge, every one turned toward the same dark edge of the sky.

Assumes: They are old machines somebody built and left, because they stand in a straight line. Really: They are alive, and they answer.

Cost: Before she knows, she chips a sample off the nearest pillar. It turns the faint pink of a bruise.

Speaker: Tess, speaking to the pillars once she understands.

You have more than you need. Now give it a shape.

📌 Watch how Jonah outlined
Jonah’s outline
Jonah · 8th Grade Scholar

1 Arrival: The lander sets down and the radio dies. / Snow squeaks; the helmet smells of warm plastic; a blue glow under the ice. / She climbs the ridge to start the map.

2 The meeting: Forty pillars, all facing the same dark edge of the sky. / “Machines,” she decides, and writes it on the map. / She chips a sample from the nearest one.

3 The change: She hums to keep warm, and the nearest pillar changes color in time. / She hums again, and it answers. The chipped one has gone the pink of a bruise. / SPEECH: Tess speaks to the pillars. She came to map them and never once asked.

4 The ending: She crosses out “machines” on her map and sits down to wait.

Held back: Why every pillar faces the same dark edge of the sky.

Each part has two or three beats, one line each.
My change has a SPEECH beat: who speaks, and the one thing they say.
