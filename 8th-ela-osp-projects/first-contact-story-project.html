First-Contact Story Project

Write a story about a first contact. At its turning point, one character gives a speech. Then perform that speech from memory.
The module question: what do we lose when we assume we understand the other?

How it is graded
 
Imitation of Lewis’s technique (sensory world-building)
ExcellentThe world is built impression by impression, through the senses in a believable order, so the reader discovers it as the traveler does.
ProficientThe world is built largely through the senses; most details arrive before they are explained.
DevelopingSome sensory detail appears, but the world is often told rather than shown.
BeginningThe world is mostly named or explained, with little sensory building.
Narrative craft (clear arc and point of view)
ExcellentA clear arc with a meaningful change; a steady, consistent point of view throughout.
ProficientA clear arc and a mostly consistent point of view.
DevelopingAn arc is present but uneven; point of view slips in places.
BeginningLittle sense of arc; point of view is unclear or shifts often.
Sensory and figurative language
ExcellentFresh, precise images and well-chosen comparisons that make the strange world vivid.
ProficientClear images and some effective figurative language.
DevelopingSome images, but often general or repeated; little figurative language.
BeginningVague or flat language; the world is hard to picture.
Clarity and correctness of prose
ExcellentClean, controlled sentences; varied structure; errors are rare and do not distract.
ProficientMostly clean sentences; minor errors that do not obscure meaning.
DevelopingSeveral run-ons, fragments, or splices that slow the reader.
BeginningFrequent errors that often make meaning unclear.
The speech: voice & style imitation
ExcellentSentence rhythm, word choice, and tone consistently sound like Lewis’s world: long, gathered sentences, restrained detail, wonder braided with moral seriousness.
ProficientThe voice mostly sounds like Lewis’s world, with occasional lines that slip into modern or casual phrasing.
DevelopingSome formal or old-fashioned phrasing appears, but much of the speech reads like ordinary modern speech.
BeginningThe speech does not attempt a formal voice; it reads as the student’s own ordinary speech with a name attached.
The speech: original content
ExcellentEvery line is the student’s own words; the speech is specific, believable for the character who gives it, and reveals something the story has not yet said.
ProficientThe content is original and fits the character; the moment is clear, if less surprising.
DevelopingThe content is original but generic; the character’s reasoning is vague.
BeginningThe speech borrows heavily from the novel’s actual lines rather than the student’s own words.
Delivery
ExcellentDelivered from memory with clear pacing, audible volume, and a voice and posture that fit the character; sounds like speech, not reading.
ProficientDelivered with only brief glances at notes; pacing and volume are mostly clear.
DevelopingDelivered mostly by reading; pacing is rushed or volume is hard to hear in places.
BeginningRead flatly with little preparation; difficult to follow as a spoken performance.
Done when
