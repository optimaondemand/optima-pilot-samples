Draft model

Golding’s island looks like a place where order could win, and then he shows how easily it slips. On the platform, Ralph “noted the coolness and shade” (Ch. 1), and the choice of words matters. Cool shade is a small comfort, so the platform feels like a safe room cut out of the glare, a place made for a meeting. That is the order at its best: calm, shaded and shared. The shade invites the boys to sit and talk. The first fire starts the same way, with a cheer and a plan. But Golding writes that the fire “began to gnaw” (Ch. 2), and gnawing is what a hungry animal does. The verb turns a rescue signal into something alive and eating the forest, and nobody is steering it. The boys who cheered the flame cannot tell it where to stop, and a cheering crowd is not the same as an order that holds. Notice that both quotations lean on the body: first comfort, then hunger. Together, the cool platform and the gnawing fire show an order that depends on the conditions staying friendly. At this point the order on the island looks fragile, because the boys build it in the shade and then lose their grip on it the moment something exciting happens.
