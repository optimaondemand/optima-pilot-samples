A Stage Made of Words

Tap each part of the drawing. Numbers point to the sources below.

The public playhouses performed in the afternoon, by daylight, with no stage lighting.1 A scene set at night was still played in the sun.
Plays used little or no scenery. The actors described the place, or a costume suggested it.1 The stage in the drawing juts out into the yard, with the audience on three sides.2, 3
The back wall has two doors. De Witt labels the building behind them mimorum aedes, the actors’ house, where players dressed and waited.3 Every entrance and exit in the script passes through a door like these.
Women did not act on the public stage. Female parts were played by boy players in women’s costume.1 Hermia, Helena, and Hippolyta were all written for boys.
The moon is in the words

Hippolyta speaks of the night of her wedding, in full afternoon light. Nothing on the stage shows the moon. Her lines do.

HippolytaFour days will quickly steep themselves in night;
Four nights will quickly dream away the time;
And then the moon, like to a silver bow
New bent in heaven, shall behold the night
Of our solemnities.(1.1)

What Theseus Leaves Out

Theseus admits he meant to speak with Demetrius about Helena, “But, being over-full of self-affairs, / My mind did lose it.” What does the admission add to his ruling?

Before she answers, Hermia asks to know “The worst that may befall me in this case, / If I refuse to wed Demetrius.” What does the request show?

Theseus takes Egeus and Demetrius aside: “I have some private schooling for you both.” What do we learn about that talk?

Alone at the end of the scene, Helena says “Love looks not with the eyes, but with the mind;”. What does she then decide to do?
