Module 7B Presentation: Your Findings, in Four Minutes
English 1 · Module 7B
25 pointsLive or recordedVisual + upload
What you present
 
Present your own findings to an audience, with something on screen to help them follow, in four to five minutes.
It is not a summary of the paper read aloud. It is the argument, made to people, in the time available.
Two ways to do it
 
PathWhat you do
Live sectionPresent to your class. Your teacher scores it in the room. Share your visual on screen as you go. Then upload your slides or visual.
On demandRecord yourself presenting, with your visual on screen or cut in. Use the Canvas recorder, your phone, or screen-recording software. Upload your recording and your visual.
Both paths are scored identically.
What to say, and in what order
 
Four to five minutes is roughly 500 to 650 words spoken. That is not enough for everything in your paper, so choose.
MinuteWhat you are doing
0:00–0:45The question. Say what you set out to find out and why anyone should care. Do not open with “my paper is about.”
0:45–1:30Your answer, stated plainly. Give away the conclusion early. A presentation is not a mystery.
1:30–3:30Two pieces of evidence, one from each of your texts, and the point where they pull apart. This is the heart; spend the most time here.
3:30–4:15The strongest objection to your reading, and your reply. Taking this on is what makes it an argument rather than a report.
4:15–5:00What it means. One sentence on what changes if you are right.
The visual
 
You must use something on screen: slides, a document camera, a diagram, a shared image of a passage.
DoDo not
Put the two quotations you are comparing side by side, so the audience can see the thing you are describingPut your script on the slides. An audience reading cannot listen.
Use an image or diagram where a shape does the explaining, such as the four-part structure, the fork, or the two-column comparisonFill slides with bullet points that repeat what you are saying
Keep it to four or five slidesRead your slides aloud
The most useful thing to put on a slide
 
The two quotations you are comparing, next to each other, with nothing else on the slide. Your audience cannot hold two passages in their head from listening alone, and the whole point of your argument is what happens between them. Show them the thing, then talk about it.
On the time limit
 
Rehearse with a timer at least twice. The fix for running long is to cut one piece of evidence, not to speak faster. Fitting the argument into the time given is part of what is scored.
Upload
 
Live: present in class, then upload your slides or visual. On demand: upload your recording and your visual. Either way, both the talk and the visual are part of the score.
