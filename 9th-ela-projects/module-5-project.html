The question
 
What can a small story hold?
Write an original short story in which one person faces a choice that costs something either way, plant details that pay off, let irony do quiet work, and end the story the way you mean to end it: resolved, twist, or deliberately open.
Your story must have all six
 
☐Length. A complete short story of 1,000 to 1,500 words.
☐A protagonist facing a real dilemma. A choice with genuine cost on both sides. If one option is obviously right, it is not a dilemma yet.
☐Deliberate foreshadowing. At least two planted details that pay off later, following your foreshadowing plan.
☐At least one form of irony: verbal, situational, or dramatic, used in service of the story.
☐A chosen ending: resolved, twist, or deliberately ambiguous. What is scored is that you chose it with intent and built the story to deserve it.
☐A craft reflection after the story: one paragraph of 150 to 250 words naming at least three techniques you used, where each appears in your story, and which studied story taught you each.
Also required
 
☐A real title
☐At least three module Word Study terms used accurately in your craft reflection
☐Your own premise, characters, and sentences: borrow techniques, never plots or words
☐A short Source Note at the end of your craft reflection: one thing you looked up, where you found it (MLA), and how it changed a detail in your story
☐Proofread before you submit

How this is scored
 
CriterionStrongDevelopingBeginning
Narrative craft
30 pts		A complete story with a protagonist a reader can believe, a dilemma with real cost on both sides, and scenes that pull their weight; the small scale feels chosen, not cramped. (30 pts)The story is complete but the dilemma is lopsided, or the protagonist is thin, or scenes wander before the story finds its shape. (26 pts)A summary or a scene fragment rather than a story; no real choice at the center; major elements missing. (20 pts)
Technique in service of story
25 pts		Foreshadowing is planted early and pays off; irony does real work on the reader; techniques feel absorbed from the studied stories, never pasted on to satisfy a checklist. (25 pts)Required techniques are present but mechanical: a planted detail that never pays off, or irony announced rather than felt. (22 pts)Required techniques are missing or unrecognizable; nothing planted, nothing paid off. (17 pts)
Ending and structure
25 pts		The ending type is clearly chosen (resolved, twist, or deliberately ambiguous) and the whole story is built to earn it; the last paragraph lands with intent. (25 pts)An ending exists but the story does not quite earn it: a twist without groundwork, or an ambiguity that reads as unfinished rather than open. (22 pts)The story stops rather than ends; no evidence of an ending chosen on purpose. (17 pts)
Craft reflection and conventions
20 pts		The reflection names three or more techniques, points to where each lives in the story, and credits the studied story that taught it; Word Study terms used well; nearly error-free. (20 pts)The reflection lists techniques without locating them in the story or crediting their sources; some errors; fewer than three terms used well. (17 pts)Reflection missing or generic; frequent errors interfere with meaning. (13 pts)
