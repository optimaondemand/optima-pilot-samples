Write Your Article

Choose one world from a text you have read. Take a role inside it. You write as that person, not as yourself, and the voice is part of what is read.

A

After the killings in the house of Atreus, the city declares that blood-vengeance will no longer settle a matter of justice, and that the household must answer to a court like anyone else. Role: a citizen of Argos.

Gawain comes home wearing the green girdle and the court laughs. The knights sit down to set out what a vow must be if human beings are actually going to keep one. Role: a named knight.

One article, written in role · Scenario B
From Sir Bedivere, Knight of the Round Table

He has required of us oaths whose keeping was never within our power, and then made the breaking of them a matter for laughter. When our brother returned bearing the girdle and named his own fault plainly before the court, we answered him with mirth and took the green belt for a badge, so that his confession was made into an ornament and his shame into a fashion (Sir Gawain 2513–18). A vow that cannot be broken is no vow; a vow whose breaking costs nothing is no vow either.

Opens with a deedNames a specific momentCarries a citationEnds on a balanced sentenceNever mentions a school

Begin the way the Declaration’s grievances begin, with a subject and a deed: He has… They have… The Machine has… Then give the specific harm. Then cite the text: a short quotation or a precise reference to the moment in the book that supports your charge.

The one rule about subject matter

Every grievance belongs to the world of the book. This is not a place for complaints about Optima, your teachers, your classes, or anyone you actually know. If your article could be true outside the book, it is in the wrong document.

My world and my role Name the world you chose and the person you are writing as.
The principle my world holds One sentence stating what that world holds to be self-evident.
My article Three or four sentences, in role. Open with a subject and a deed, name a specific harm, and cite the text.
Check your article
  • It is written in role, in three or four sentences.
  • It opens with a subject and a deed, and names a specific harm.
  • It cites the text: a short quotation or a precise reference to the moment.
  • Everything in it belongs to the world of the book.

Your Thesis

“The world will little note nor long remember what we say here, but it can never forget what they did here.”Gettysburg Address · 1863
First half: what we say. It will be forgotten.Second half: what they did. It will not.

The halves match in shape and set one thing against another. That makes a balanced sentence, and it is a good shape for a thesis: one sentence, two parts set against each other, that could only be written by someone holding both texts.

A thesis frame

Although [the document] promises ___, [the story] shows ___; together they argue that ___.

Model · Gettysburg and The Scarlet Letter

Lincoln stands inside the standard he asks the nation to meet, but Hawthorne’s town enforces a law it exempts its own leaders from, so a standard means nothing unless its makers accept it too.

Which thesis could only be written by someone holding both texts?

My thesis One sentence that answers your question. Use the frame if it helps, and break the frame if your idea needs it.
  • A reasonable person could disagree with it.
  • It needs both of my texts.

Your Counterargument

1

State it fairly

In words its holder would accept.

2

Say why it is strong

What it gets right.

3

Answer it

Where it falls short, using your texts or a source.

The opposite of fair is a straw man: the other side stated weakly so it is easy to knock down. Placing the counterargument late works well. Leaving it out costs you the top band. It runs 150 to 200 words.

Which opening states the objection fairly?

The strongest objection to my reading Write it so someone who believed it would say yes, that is what I think.
Who would hold this view, and why One or two sentences. A historian, a character, a reader, or a source you found.
My reply Answer it with your evidence. Concede what is fair, and say where it falls short.
F

Your Conclusion

Model conclusion opening · Gettysburg and The Scarlet Letter

If a standard means nothing unless its makers accept it, then a reader should ask of any founding promise not what it says but who is willing to be measured by it. Lincoln answers by speaking as one of the people under test. Boston never does.

Which conclusion does the job?

My conclusion 100 to 150 words. If a reader believed your thesis, what would they see differently?
A

Run the Checks

Put your paper together in Word in this order: introduction, bodies, counterargument, conclusion. Then work through these checks on the real document.

  • Cover test. For every body paragraph, cover one text. If the point still stands, add the missing text into the claim.
  • Question and thesis. My question is stated in the introduction, and my thesis answers it.
  • Quotations. I opened the text and checked every quotation word for word. Each sits inside a sentence of my own and is followed by what it shows.
  • Sources. I have four, in the required mix, each doing work. Every idea that is not mine is cited, including ideas I put in my own words.
  • Length. 1,200 to 1,500 words, not counting the Works Cited page.

The most useful thing to put on a slide

The two quotations you are comparing, next to each other, with nothing else on the slide. Your audience cannot hold two passages in their head from listening alone, and the whole point of your argument is what happens between them. Show them the thing, then talk about it.
