Find One Source

What countsOne source beyond the novel, about Puritan New England, the world the novel is set in.
PreferredA scholarly article from JSTOR, a library of articles written by researchers. Your school gives you free access; your teacher can tell you where to find the login.
Open itRead the source yourself before you write about it. A title and a summary are not enough.
Keep two worlds apartA source tells you about the period it studies. What happens in Boston in the book belongs to the novel: write “in the novel” when you describe it.

Three Quotations

One entry, for shape · a sample on a different claim
ClaimHawthorne treats revenge as a wound the avenger inflicts on himself.
Quotation“A mortal man, with once a human heart, has become a fiend for his especial torment!” (Hawthorne, ch. 14)
ProofChillingworth admits that revenge has turned a man “with once a human heart” into a fiend, so the avenger is the one most changed by the revenge.

Counterclaim · 100–150 words

Plan the counterclaim Three notes. The strongest view against your thesis, stated so fairly that someone who holds it would nod. What is true in it (begin “It is true that”). Your rebuttal, pointing to a moment or chapter (begin “But”).

Plan the conclusion Whatever prompt you chose, your conclusion answers this: what does a community owe the person it judges, and what does a person owe the truth? Say what your essay’s answer is, in two or three sentences. Do not start over with a new idea.
