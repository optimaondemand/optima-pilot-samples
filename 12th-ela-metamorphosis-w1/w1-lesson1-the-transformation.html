Kafka’s World, 1883–1924

Tap each year. Numbers point to the sources below.

Franz Kafka is born on July 3, 1883, near the Old Town Square in Prague, into a German-speaking, middle-class Jewish family. His father, Hermann, runs a shop selling haberdashery and ladies’ accessories.1
He is awarded the degree of Doctor of Law on June 18, 1906.1 At university he has met Max Brod, who becomes a friend for life.1
After a year at a private insurance firm with hours that leave little time to write, he joins the Worker’s Accident Insurance Institute for the Kingdom of Bohemia. His job is to investigate and assess compensation for injuries to industrial workers.1 He writes in the hours the job leaves him.
In August he meets Felice Bauer. On the night of September 22 he writes the story “The Judgment” in one sitting.1 In the same productive stretch, that year, he writes Die Verwandlung, the story English readers call The Metamorphosis.1
The story appears in the October issue of the journal Die weißen Blätter, then as a book in December, in the Kurt Wolff series Der jüngste Tag.2, 3 Writing to his publisher about the cover that October, Kafka asks that the insect itself not be drawn, and not be shown even from a distance.2
Ill with tuberculosis since 1917, he dies on June 3, 1924, at a sanatorium near Vienna, aged 40.1 He had asked Max Brod to destroy his remaining work. Brod ignored the request and published it.1 The word Kafkaesque now names situations like those in his books, where a person is overpowered by forces that make no sense.1

Scene Map

Open a scene to check your reading. Each is told in our words.

1Waking

Gregor finds his body changed: a hard, plated back, a domed belly, many thin legs. The room around him is ordinary, his own, and the picture he framed still hangs on the wall.

2The job

He complains to himself about the traveling life and the boss. He would quit, but his parents owe the boss money, and he expects five or six more years of work to clear it.

3Voices at the doors

His mother, then his father, then his sister speak to him through three different doors of his room. He answers, and his voice sounds wrong to him. His doors are locked from inside.

4The chief clerk

Soon after seven the firm’s chief clerk arrives at the flat. Through the locked door he raises suspicions about Gregor and his work, in front of the parents.

5The speech

Gregor answers with a long, anxious speech promising to catch a later train. The clerk hears only a voice he calls an animal’s. The sister and the maid are sent for help.

6The door opens

Gregor turns the key with his mouth. The clerk backs away, the mother sinks down, and the father, after weeping, drives Gregor back through the door with the clerk’s stick. Gregor is hurt in the doorway, and the door slams.
