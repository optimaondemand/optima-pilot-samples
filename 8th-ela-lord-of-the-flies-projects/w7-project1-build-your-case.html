  1. The card. Read the strongest objection to your side.
  2. Your log. Which moment points the other way?

The strongest objection to each position
Against The worse part wins

A reader could say: some boys keep choosing the better thing even when it costs them. If the worse part always wins, how do you explain them? And is it the worse part of human nature, or only the worse part of fear?

Against The better part survives in some

A reader could say: the boys who keep their goodness cannot stop what happens, and most of the island goes the other way. Can a better part that protects no one honestly be called what is left of us?

Against It depends on the person

A reader could say: “it depends” dodges the question. The island changes almost every boy it touches, so if most go the same way, isn’t that the real answer about us?

Objection 1: what a thoughtful reader would say against you

📌 Watch how Jonah answered an objection
Jonah’s objection
Jonah · 8th Grade Scholar

Objection: A reader could say that it depends dodges the question, because the island changes almost every boy it touches.

Answer: The question asks what is left of us, and the novel answers it boy by boy. When Simon says, “maybe it’s only us” (Ch. 5), he is the only one willing to look for the beast in the boys themselves, and the assembly laughs him down. The same island gives Simon honesty and gives the hunters noise. If the island decided, they would end up alike. They do not, so the difference has to come from the person.
