Jonah’s introduction
Jonah · 8th Grade Scholar

When the structures of civilization fall away, what is left of us: the better part or the worse? Lord of the Flies does not answer with one boy or one scene. It answers boy by boy. When civilization falls away, what is left depends on the person: Simon keeps trying to tell the truth, while Roger finds out how little was holding him back. The island is the same for both of them. What differs is what each one carries onto it.

2Evidence 1

Try this: make a claim, bring in the moment or a short quotation, then say what the words do. A few words you explain beat a long line you do not.

📌 Watch how Jonah wrote an evidence paragraph
Roger and Henry
Jonah · 8th Grade Scholar

Roger shows how thin the old life can become. In Chapter 4 he throws stones near a small boy named Henry, but he throws them to miss. Golding says why: “Roger’s arm was conditioned by a civilization that knew nothing of him and was in ruins” (Ch. 4). The word “conditioned” makes his restraint sound like a habit, not a choice, and “in ruins” tells us the habit has little time left. Roger already kicks over the littluns’ sandcastles, but he cannot yet hit a child; he is held back only by what remains of parents, school, and the law. That matters for my thesis, because it shows the worse part is not forced on anyone by the island. For Roger it is already waiting, and only a habit keeps it quiet.

Notice: claim first, then the moment, then the quotation led into and cited by chapter, then what the words do.

Three moves for each objection
  1. State it fairly. In words its owner would accept.
  2. Answer it. A mistake, outweighed, or granted and held.
  3. Close the essay. What your answer says about people.

📌 Watch how Jonah wrote an objection and an answer
Jonah’s objection paragraph
Jonah · 8th Grade Scholar

A reader could object that it depends dodges the question, because the island changes almost every boy it touches. That objection deserves respect, since most of the boys do change. But the question asks what is left of us, and the novel answers it boy by boy. When Simon says, “maybe it’s only us” (Ch. 5), he is the only one willing to look for the beast in the boys themselves, and the assembly laughs him down. The same island gives Simon honesty and gives the hunters noise. If the island decided, they would end up alike. They do not, so the difference has to come from the person.

Notice: the objection is stated fairly, granted in part, and then answered with evidence.

3Close the essay

📌 Watch how Jonah closed
Jonah’s conclusion
Jonah · 8th Grade Scholar

So what is left of us? Not one thing. The island does not decide; it asks, and each boy answers with what he brought. That is a harder answer than the worse part wins, and a more hopeful one, because it means the answer is partly ours.
