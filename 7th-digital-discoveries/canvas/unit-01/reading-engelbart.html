Engelbart's "Mother of All Demos"

Our anchor for the unit — and the clips behind the Lesson 4 discussion.

Watch the demo

On December 9, 1968, Douglas Engelbart stood in front of a roomful of computer scientists and, in one 90-minute live demonstration, showed the future: the mouse, on-screen windows, hyperlinks, video conferencing, and people editing the same document together. It was so far ahead of its time that people later called it "The Mother of All Demos." Watch it free at the official Doug Engelbart Institute:

What to watch (you do not watch all 90 minutes)

The full demo is long and technical. Watch the short highlights version the Institute provides (about 10–12 minutes) — or, if you want more, the annotated version lets you jump to chapters. As you watch, look especially for these moments:

  • the first public use of a computer mouse to move a pointer on screen;
  • Engelbart editing text on a screen — cutting, pasting, and reorganizing a list;
  • clicking a link to jump from one place to another (hyperlinks, decades before the Web);
  • two people in different places working on the same screen at once.
A few words to know
  • augment — to add to or strengthen. Engelbart's goal was "augmenting human intellect" — using computers to make people's thinking more powerful.
  • adaptive technology — tools designed so that everyone, including people with disabilities, can use a computer.
  • demo — a live demonstration that shows a new tool working in front of an audience.
Watch with these questions in hand
  1. What did Engelbart understand about people that made his machine so different?
  2. Pick one specific moment in the clips. What did it let a person do that was new?
  3. What is one adaptive technology today that continues his goal of helping every person?
