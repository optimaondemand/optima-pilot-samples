What Is Computing?

Where does computing live — and where did the very idea of it begin?

Computing is all around you

When you hear the word computer, you might picture a laptop. But computing is far wider than that. It is in the phone that wakes you, the traffic light that times the road, the game that responds to your hands, the music that streams to your ears. Computing is what happens when we give a machine a clear set of instructions and it carries them out — faithfully, quickly, and without tiring.

This course treats computer science as the discipline of ordered thought: a way of solving problems, communicating, and creating. You will not only use computers — you will learn to think the way they require, and to make them do useful and beautiful things.

The oldest idea in computing: the algorithm

Long before electricity, people had the central idea of computing: a step-by-step procedure that anyone can follow to reach a result. Around the year 820, a scholar named Muḥammad al-Khwārizmī wrote books on solving problems with orderly steps. His name, carried into Latin, became our word algorithm — a precise sequence of steps that solves a problem. (His book al-Jabr gave us the word algebra, which we will meet again later this year.)

An algorithm is not a machine. It is an idea — and a very old one, belonging to the same liberal-arts tradition as geometry and grammar. A recipe is an algorithm. The steps to long division are an algorithm. This year you will learn to read, write, and trace algorithms with care.

Key words
  • Computing — giving a machine clear instructions to carry out a task.
  • Computer science — the study of how we solve problems and create with computing.
  • Algorithm — a precise, step-by-step procedure that solves a problem.

Unplugged Write the algorithm In plain words, write the step-by-step algorithm for an everyday task — making a sandwich, tying a shoe. Then ask someone at home to follow your steps exactly, doing nothing you did not write — or set the steps aside and follow them yourself tomorrow. What broke? Computers, like your helper, do only what they are told.
