The Machine & the Human

Essential question: What is a computer, and how do we meet it halfway?

Before we can command a computer, we must understand what it is. In this first unit we open the case and look inside — at the hardware we could touch and the software that brings it to life — and we study the meeting place between the machine and the person using it. A computer is powerful, but it is not wise; it does exactly what it is told. Learning to use one well begins with knowing what we are speaking to.

Anchor text — our great book for this unit Douglas Engelbart's "The Mother of All Demos" (1968): the first public demonstration of the mouse, on-screen windows, and people working together through a screen. We will watch clips and ask: what did Engelbart understand about people that made his machine so different?

Our virtue this unit — Responsibility A tool is only as good as the care of the one who wields it. As we learn what a computer is, we ask what it means to use it responsibly — and whether the work of our hands is good, true, and beautiful.

How you will be assessed

Short checks for understanding along the way, and a Unit 1 assessment: a brief quiz plus a short written explanation of how the hardware and software in a device you use work together.
