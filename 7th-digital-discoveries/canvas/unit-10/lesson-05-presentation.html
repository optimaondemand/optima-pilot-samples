Unit 10 · Lesson 5

The Presentation

Rhetoric's last lesson: say what you made, in your own voice, and mean it.

Your recorded presentation

Your capstone ends with a recorded presentation — 2 to 4 minutes, in your own voice, screen-sharing or filming your portfolio. There is no live audience to fear; there is only the craft of saying it well. A shape that works:

  1. Welcome (± 20 seconds) — who you are as a maker; what this portfolio holds.
  2. Two artifacts, closely (± 1 minute each) — for each: what it is, the skill it shows, and the moment it taught you something (a bug conquered, a hypothesis refuted, a trace that finally balanced).
  3. The year in one thought (± 30 seconds) — from al-Khwārizmī to Berners-Lee, what do you now believe about computing that you did not believe in August?

Voice and tone matter: speak as you would to a respected guest — warm, precise, unhurried. Practice once, record twice, keep the better take.

The final reflection

With your presentation you will submit the year's last written reflection — the same question we have asked every unit, now asked of the whole: Was the work of your year good, true, and beautiful — and which virtue did it demand most of you?

Hands-on: The canon, one last walk

Before you speak of your own work, honor the thinkers who came before. Match each to their idea.

    The year's last reflection — Courage & service You began this course learning what a computer is. You end it as someone who commands one — honestly, precisely, and for good purposes. Present your work with courage. Watch your classmates' recordings with generosity. And carry the question with you: is it good, true, and beautiful?
    Before you record
    1. Does your portfolio open cleanly from a fresh browser tab?
    2. Have you practiced your three-part shape once, out loud?
    3. Is your final reflection honest — the hard parts included?
