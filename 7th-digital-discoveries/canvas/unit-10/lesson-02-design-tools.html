Unit 10 · Lesson 2

The Craftsman's Tools

A design tool amplifies your judgment — it cannot replace it.

Creating online content

Online content means webpages, digital portfolios, and multimedia artifacts — words, images, audio, and video woven together and published. Modern design tools do the HTML for you: a school-approved site builder (such as Google Sites) lets you place headings, images, and links visually, and a slide tool can build a portfolio page by page. Because these tools are collaborative, a team can build one artifact together — the same multimedia collaboration you practiced in Unit 2, now aimed at publishing.

Design that serves the reader

Tools offer a thousand options; craft is knowing which to refuse. Four rules carry you far:

  • Headings in order — one clear title, then sections; a page is an outline the reader can scan.
  • Contrast that can be read — dark text on light (or the reverse); never pale-on-pale. Every reader benefits; some depend on it.
  • Alt text on every image — say what the picture shows, for those who cannot see it.
  • Less, but better — one typeface family, a few colors, no blinking clutter. Beauty is restraint.

Notice these are the same rules this course itself follows — and the same accessibility principles from Unit 1. Good design is courtesy, extended to every reader.
