Polish & Peers

Good feedback is a gift twice: to the receiver's work and the giver's eye.

Warm and cool feedback

This week your classmates read your draft portfolio (on the discussion board) and you read theirs. We use a classical pair of lenses:

  • Warm feedback — name specifically what works: "Your simulation introduction states the hypothesis so clearly I could predict your chart before I saw it."
  • Cool feedback — a courteous, specific question or suggestion: "Could the welcome page say which artifact you're proudest of? I wanted to know where to start."

Both must be specific ("I like it" helps no one) and about the work (never the person). Receiving feedback has its own discipline: thank the giver, decide with your own judgment, and revise on purpose.

Where these skills lead — one last look

In Unit 2 you analyzed a technology career. Look again with a year's more skill in hand. The maker of your portfolio has practiced, this year, the daily work of: web developers (online content, design, accessibility), software developers (loops, debugging, the life cycle), data and simulation scientists (hypotheses, models, honest findings), and UX designers (design that serves the reader). The Bureau of Labor Statistics' Occupational Outlook Handbook will tell you what each path asks and offers — you now speak enough of their language to read it well.
