Give a thorough presentation and explanation. Feel free to use historical photographs, charts, and stock footage in addition to any immersive media elements.

Topic

One real case

Choose one real historical or contemporary case that shows how factors of production (land, labor, capital, entrepreneurship) combine within an economic system to respond to scarcity and pursue broad economic goals. Examples include the Industrial Revolution, a command economy such as the Soviet Union or Cuba, a market economy responding to a resource shock, or a side-by-side comparison of two real nations with different economic systems. A good presentation will touch on factors of production, the economic system(s) involved, and the broad economic goals (efficiency, growth, freedom, fairness, stability) at stake.

Draft Your Thesis

Lesson 1.01 · scarcity and the three questions, in your words
Frame

In [case], [system] answered scarcity by [how the factors were combined], and it [advanced or gave up which goals] because [reason].

Your thesis One or two sentences. Use the frame or leave it. It can change; it cannot be blank.
A thesis picks a side on a question the case could answer either way. Ardena (made up): "Its planners hit the growth goal and gave up the fairness goal" can be argued. "Ardena had planners" cannot.
Test it
  • It names my case.
  • It says how the system combined the factors to answer scarcity.
  • It names at least one goal and what pursuing it cost.
  • Someone could reasonably argue against it.

Three Kinds of Evidence

Primary source

Made at the time you are studying. It shows what people saw, wrote, or counted then.

Library of Congress

Scholar

Written later by someone who studied the case and argues what it meant. It gives you an argument to agree with, correct, or answer.

JStor

Data series

Numbers recorded over time. A chart of one tells the viewer what changed and when.

FRED · BLS · Census

General encyclopedias and unsigned web pages are places to start looking for names to search. They are not sources to cite.

Vocab

Subject vocabulary
IncentiveA reward or reason that pushes people toward one choice over another.
Opportunity CostThe value of the next-best choice you give up when you decide.
ScarcityThe condition of having limited resources while human wants have no limit.
Trade-OffGiving up one thing in order to get another.
EntrepreneurshipThe drive to start something new, take risks, and combine land, labor, and capital into a working business.
Human CapitalThe skills, knowledge, and experience that make workers more productive.
Physical CapitalHuman-made tools, such as machines, buildings, and roads, that are used to produce goods.
ThesisOne sentence that states your answer and that someone could argue against. The rest of the project proves it.
Primary SourceSomething made at the time you are studying, such as a photograph, a map, or an archive document.
Secondary SourceWork by someone who studied the case later and argues what it means, such as a journal article.
Data SeriesNumbers recorded over time, such as prices or employment, that you can chart.
CitationA short note in the text, and a full entry in the reference list, that tells the viewer where a fact or an image came from.
