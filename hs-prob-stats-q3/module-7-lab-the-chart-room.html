Why this lab

This module's virtue is honesty. A graph can tell the truth in every number and still mislead, and a defensive reader asks five questions of every graph:

  1. Zero start? For bars and pictographs, does the axis begin at zero?
  2. Full range? Is the whole time range shown, or only a convenient slice?
  3. Equal gaps? Are equal gaps on the axis equal intervals of the variable?
  4. Sizes true? Do the sizes of icons and bars match the values (length for bars, same-size icons for counts)?
  5. Labels, source? Are the title, units, sample size, bin width, and source given?

Today you need two of them most. Zero start? catches bars whose bottoms have been cut off. Sizes true? catches bars or icons that grow faster than the counts they stand for.

Today you build the evidence yourself. You build a bar chart out of identical tokens, one token for every vegetable in a class garden's harvest, then reshape the same tokens into a circle graph and find out why a slice's angle is exactly its share of 360°. Then comes the unusual part: you deliberately make the bar chart lie, one change and no number altered, and you label your own lie. A lie you have made with your own hands is a lie you will spot on sight. A lie that is labeled is a lesson; an unlabeled one is a lie, so the page will not let you leave the dishonest chart without its label.

Step 5

Make the bar chart lie, with one change only. Choose one of these, and change no token count.

A · The cut-off axis. Slide the Floor Cover up the chart until it hides the bottom token of every column, so the chart appears to start above zero.
