Walk the numbers, do not just read them

This module's virtue is courtesy. A number you hand to a reader should fit the data it stands for, and you owe the reader an honest word about what it leaves out. Today you do not read summary numbers off a page: you walk them. Fifteen data values stand as tokens on a numbered strip. You will stand on the mean, pace out one standard deviation each way, count how many tokens fall inside, place the median and the quartiles, and measure the gap from the median to the mean. Then you tell the story of the distribution: which way it leans, what pulled the mean, which pair of summaries you would hand a reader, and one thing every summary hides. A skew you have walked is a skew you will recognize on sight. All the data in this lab are constructed (made up).

Have these three ideas ready

  1. Quartiles by hand. Q1 is the median of the lower half and Q3 is the median of the upper half. The median itself is left out of both halves.
  2. Sample standard deviation. Divide the sum of squared deviations by n − 1, not by n. This is the default here.
  3. Skew. A long tail of high values pulls the mean up and to the right of the median.

Step 5 · The Median and the Quartiles

Time≈ 7 min
Status·

The median is the middle token. With 15 tokens it is the 8th from the left. Q1 is the median of the 7 tokens to its left and Q3 is the median of the 7 tokens to its right, the by-hand method: the median itself is left out of both halves. The IQR is the distance from Q1 to Q3.
