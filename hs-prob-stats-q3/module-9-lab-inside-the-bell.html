What you are doing

The virtue here is wonder. A ball dropped through a board of pegs goes left or right at every peg, and a thousand small left-or-right choices pile up into a bell. That bell is nearly the same bell wherever it appears. Today you stand inside one. It has twelve towers, each standing over a half-unit slice of the z-ruler, with the number of made-up bean plants in each tower posted on it. You walk the ruler from z = −2 to z = +2, place three banners that mark the Empirical Rule's bands, add up what is inside each, and compare your percents with 68, 95, and 99.7. Then two made-up visitors arrive with one measurement each, and you find where each one stands and how many plants stand beyond. Finally you tell the story, including one thing that this bell, as lovely as it is, cannot tell you. All of the data in this lab are constructed (made up).

Two ideas to carry A z-score counts standard deviations from the mean: z = (x − μ) ÷ σ. Solved for the measurement, x = μ + zσ. The Empirical Rule says that in a normal distribution about 68% of values lie within 1 standard deviation of the mean, about 95% within 2, and about 99.7% within 3. The made-up bean plants in this lab have a mean of 50 cm and a standard deviation of 8 cm.
