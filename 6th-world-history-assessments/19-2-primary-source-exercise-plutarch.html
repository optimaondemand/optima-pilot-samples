Passage
Lycurgus made a rule that only iron money should be used. He gave it a small value for its great weight, so that a sum of ten minas needed a large storeroom in the house and a yoke of oxen to carry it. When this money became the custom, many kinds of wrongdoing left Sparta. For who would steal it, or take it as a bribe, or rob for it, when it could not be hidden, could not be enjoyed, and could not even be cut up to any profit?
Plutarch, Life of Lycurgus, chapter 9 (adapted from a public-domain translation). Plutarch wrote many hundreds of years after the time of Lycurgus.
Question 1

Plutarch says iron money reduced wrongdoing in Sparta. Explain the reasoning behind his claim. Then use the lesson “Sparta: A Polis Built for War” to describe one other effect that iron money may have had on Sparta, even though Plutarch does not mention it here.

Question 2

Plutarch admits that no one agrees when Lycurgus lived, and he wrote long after. Choose one thing in this passage that you think we can reasonably trust and one thing we should question. Explain your thinking for each, and say what else you would want to know. Use what you learned in the lesson “Sparta: A Polis Built for War” to support your answer.
