WEEK 19 · READING QUIZ
Reading Quiz: Mountains and Sea
5 questions · 5 points · one attempt
Before you start

Choose the one best answer for each question.

QUESTION 1
In Egypt and Mesopotamia, strong rulers gathered many towns under one crown. Why was that much harder to do in Greece?
A. Greek farmers did not want to pay taxes to any ruler.
B. Mountains and islands split the land into small, separate areas, and there were no wide plains for an army to cross.
C. Greece had too few people to fill an army.
D. The Greeks did not have a common language.
QUESTION 2
A Greek traveler would say, "I am a citizen of Corinth," not "I am a citizen of Greece." What does this best show?
A. Greeks did not care about their gods or festivals.
B. Greeks were ashamed of being Greek.
C. Corinth was the largest city in Greece.
D. A person's loyalty belonged first to his own city-state, not to a larger country.
QUESTION 3
Hesiod quarreled with his brother over land and then wrote a poem praising justice. What can we infer about why he valued justice?
A. He had seen how much a farmer needs fair judges and honest neighbors in order to prosper.
B. He wanted to become a famous warrior like Achilles.
C. He believed that only kings could be fair.
D. He thought justice mattered less than glory.
QUESTION 4
Hoplites bought their own armor. Why might a farmer-soldier begin to ask for a voice in running his city?
A. Armor made him too heavy to farm.
B. He was a noble who wanted to be a king.
C. He risked his life and paid his own costs to defend the city, so he felt he had earned a say.
D. The city law required every hoplite to join the government.
QUESTION 5
Settlers from a Greek city found a colony in Sicily. Which description best fits the new colony?
A. A province governed directly by officials from the mother city.
B. A new polis with its own laws and citizens that often kept friendly ties with the mother city.
C. A temporary camp that was abandoned after a year.
D. A city ruled by the king of Persia.
