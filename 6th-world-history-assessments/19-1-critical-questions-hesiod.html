Excerpt 1
So there was never just one kind of Strife. There are two on the earth. One a man would praise when he understood her, and the other he would blame, for they have quite different hearts. One makes ugly war and fighting grow. She is cruel, and no man loves her, but men honor this heavy Strife because the gods require it. The other is the elder daughter of dark Night. She stirs even the helpless man to work. A man grows eager for work when he looks at his neighbor, a rich man who hurries to plant and sow and put his house in good order. Neighbor competes with neighbor as he hurries after wealth. This Strife is good for men.
Hesiod, Works and Days, lines 11 to 24 (adapted from a public-domain translation)
Question 1

Hesiod says there are two kinds of Strife, which is the Greek idea of contest. Using what you learned in the lesson “Mountains and Sea: Why Greece Became Many Cities”, explain how Hesiod's good Strife differs from the contest Homer's heroes pursued, and why a city might want one kind and fear the other.

Excerpt 2
But those who give straight judgments to strangers and to their own people, and never turn aside from what is just, their city blooms, and the people in it flourish. Famine never keeps company with men who judge straight. But for those who practice violence and cruel deeds, Zeus, who sees from far away, appoints a punishment. Often even a whole city suffers because of one bad man.
Hesiod, Works and Days, lines 225 to 231 and 240 to 241 (adapted from a public-domain translation)
Question 2

Imagine that the judges of a Greek polis began taking gifts from rich men and ruling in their favor. Using Hesiod's words and what you know about his quarrel with his brother, explain what Hesiod would expect to happen to that city, and what an ordinary farmer might do. Use what you learned in the lesson “Mountains and Sea: Why Greece Became Many Cities” to support your answer. Your verdict

Question 3

Hesiod believes a city lives or dies by whether its judges are fair. Do you agree? Take a side, and defend it using Hesiod and the lesson “Mountains and Sea: Why Greece Became Many Cities”. Say what the strongest argument against your view would be.
