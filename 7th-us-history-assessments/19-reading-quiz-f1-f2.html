WEEK 19 · READING QUIZ
Reading Quiz: Lessons F.1 and F.2
7 questions · 7 points · 2 attempts
Before you start

This quiz covers Hamilton Builds a National Economy; Jefferson Organizes the Opposition and Adams Avoids War with France; Federalists Silence Their Critics. You may take it twice, and your higher score counts. Some questions ask you to put two or more facts together, so think before you answer.

QUESTION 1
Virginia had already paid most of its war debts by 1790. Based on this, which reaction to Hamilton's assumption plan would you expect from many Virginians?
A. They would welcome it, because it would lower their taxes to nothing.
B. They would ignore it, because it affected only the national government.
C. They would object, because their state would help pay other states' debts.
D. They would demand it, because Virginia was owed money by the other states.
QUESTION 2
In 1794, farmers in western Pennsylvania attacked tax collectors. Which fact about their lives best explains why the whiskey tax hurt them more than most Americans?
A. Hauling grain over the mountains cost too much, so they turned it into whiskey, which they also used like money.
B. They were mostly bankers who owned shares in the Bank of the United States.
C. They had fought for Britain in the Revolution and were taxed as a punishment.
D. They lived in cities where whiskey was the main product sold in stores.
QUESTION 3
Hamilton admitted the Constitution never mentions a national bank, yet he said Congress could create one. Which idea did his argument depend on?
A. The President may do anything that Congress has not forbidden.
B. The states had agreed to let Congress copy the banks of Britain.
C. The Bill of Rights lets citizens vote on any new government agency.
D. Congress may use powers not listed if they help carry out powers that are listed.
QUESTION 4
In 1793, a merchant whose business depended on trade with Britain would most likely have supported which group, and why?
A. Republicans, because they sympathized with Britain.
B. Federalists, because Hamilton and his friends favored Britain, the nation's biggest trading partner.
C. Republicans, because Jefferson wanted the Bank to help merchants.
D. Neither group, because Washington's neutrality banned all trade with Britain.
QUESTION 5
Jefferson and Madison believed the Sedition Act broke the Constitution, but they did not take the question to the Supreme Court. What was the best reason?
A. The Supreme Court had not yet been created.
B. The Constitution forbids the Court from hearing laws about speech.
C. Jefferson was afraid that Washington would overrule the Court.
D. The Court was full of Federalists, so they expected it to side with the law.
QUESTION 6
Which person would have been most likely to be charged under the Sedition Act of 1798?
A. A Republican newspaper editor who wrote harsh attacks on President Adams.
B. A Federalist newspaper editor who praised President Adams.
C. A French diplomat who refused to pay a bribe.
D. A sailor who criticized the British navy in a letter to his family.
QUESTION 7
The fight over the Bank and the fight over the Sedition Act were different disputes. What question lay underneath both of them?
A. Which party had more members in Congress?
B. Whether the nation should side with Britain or France.
C. How much power the Constitution really gives the federal government.
D. Whether Washington should serve a third term.
