WEEK 20 · READING QUIZ
Reading Quiz: Lessons F.3 and F.4
7 questions · 7 points · 2 attempts
Before you start

This quiz covers Jefferson Wins the Revolution of 1800 and Marshall Builds a Strong Court and a Strong Nation. You may take it twice, and your higher score counts. Some questions ask you to put two or more facts together, so think before you answer.

QUESTION 1
In the election of 1800 the Republicans clearly wanted Jefferson to be President, yet Jefferson and Burr tied with 73 votes each. What caused the tie?
A. Several Federalist electors voted for Burr to confuse the count.
B. Electors cast two votes with no separate vote for Vice President, and every Republican elector voted for both men.
C. The House of Representatives had already voted and could not agree.
D. Burr refused to accept the Vice Presidency and asked for a recount.
QUESTION 2
Hamilton disliked Jefferson's ideas, yet he urged Federalists in the House to support Jefferson over Burr. Which reason best explains his choice?
A. He hoped Jefferson would bring back the whiskey tax.
B. He had promised Adams that he would help Jefferson win.
C. He wanted to end the Twelfth Amendment as soon as possible.
D. He thought Burr had no principles and put the safety of the republic ahead of his party.
QUESTION 3
The three-fifths compromise was a formula for dividing up House seats and taxes among the states. How did it matter to the election of 1800?
A. It added seats and electors to the slaveholding states, which may have helped Jefferson defeat Adams.
B. It gave every state the same number of electors, which caused the tie.
C. It let each state decide whether to count its free citizens.
D. It was repealed in 1800, so it had no effect on the election.
QUESTION 4
In Marbury v. Madison, Marbury did not get his commission, yet people say Marshall won the larger fight. Why?
A. He forced Madison to deliver every commission Adams had signed.
B. He convinced Jefferson to pardon the midnight judges.
C. He gave up a small power but claimed judicial review, the power to say a law is unconstitutional.
D. He ruled that the Supreme Court could be overruled by the President.
QUESTION 5
Jefferson did not like the way Marshall's Court claimed the final word on the Constitution. Which belief would Jefferson most likely have held?
A. Only state governors can decide what the Constitution allows.
B. Each branch of government, not just the Court, can judge what the Constitution allows.
C. The Constitution should not be explained by anyone after it is ratified.
D. The Supreme Court should be abolished and replaced by Congress.
QUESTION 6
Maryland tried to tax the Baltimore branch of the Bank of the United States. Using Marshall's reasoning in McCulloch v. Maryland, why would that tax fail?
A. Banks are never allowed to be taxed.
B. The Bank was owned by Maryland's own citizens.
C. Congress had no power to create the Bank in the first place.
D. The power to tax can become the power to destroy, and federal law is supreme over state law.
QUESTION 7
A merchant in 1824 wants to ship goods by steamboat from New Jersey to New York. Which result of Gibbons v. Ogden helps the merchant most?
A. Each state may now set its own steamboat rules.
B. Only New York may license steamboats on its waters.
C. Congress controls trade between the states, so one set of federal rules replaces many state rules.
D. Steamboats are not considered part of commerce.
