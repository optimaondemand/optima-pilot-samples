The Capstone

What you are doing

All quarter you have followed a young republic as it made choices about money, land, power, and freedom. Each choice settled one question and opened a bigger one. For this Capstone you will pick one turning point and build a dossier about it: a short, organized Word document that explains what happened, shows how it could have gone another way, and traces what it left unresolved.

Choose one turning point

  • Hamilton's plan for the national bank and the debt
  • The Alien and Sedition Acts and the election of 1800
  • Marbury v. Madison and McCulloch v. Maryland
  • The Louisiana Purchase
  • The War of 1812 and the Monroe Doctrine
  • Clay's American System
  • Indian removal under Jackson
  • The Missouri Compromise
  • The Nullification Crisis
  • The annexation of Texas and the Mexican War

What your dossier must include (700 to 1,000 words, plus the table)

  1. The Situation (about 150 words). What problem did leaders face, and who wanted what?
  2. The Decision (about 200 words). What did they do, and why? Name at least two people and what each one argued.
  3. A Timeline Table. A Word table with at least five rows (Date, Event, Why it mattered).
  4. Two Sources. Quote or closely describe two primary sources from this quarter's lessons or Primary Source pages. For each, say who wrote it, for whom, and what it can and cannot tell us.
  5. The Road Not Taken (about 200 words). Pick one point where a different choice was truly possible. What might have happened, and what would have made it hard?
  6. What It Left Unresolved (about 150 words). What question did this turning point leave open that a later generation had to face?
  7. What I Am Still Unsure About (2 to 3 sentences). Name something the sources cannot settle or where historians disagree.

Working alone or in a group

You can work alone. If you chose "Yes" to group work in your Module Intro, you may team up with one or two classmates and submit one shared dossier. A group of two adds 300 words; a group of three adds 500. Put every member's name at the top. Each member also writes a 2 to 3 sentence note at the end saying what they contributed. Group members are scored with the same rubric. One member submits the file for the group.
